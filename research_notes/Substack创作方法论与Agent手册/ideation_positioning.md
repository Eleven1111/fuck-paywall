## Q1. How do they find and select ideas? (reader questions, news pegs, backlogs, pillars, series)

### Takeaway
Three engines repeat across creators:
1. **A reader-question backlog**, ranked by the writer's own excitement (Lenny, Oster, Yglesias's mailbag, Gioia's "Open Mic").
2. **A daily news peg read through one fixed lens** (HCR: a historian's lens; Scanlon: "how does this affect you").
3. **A thesis or "big idea" engine** that feeds long essays (Packy).

The concrete, repeatable mechanics are a ranked backlog, a voting thread and a morning scan.

### Cited Findings
**Lenny Rachitsky (Lenny's Newsletter)**
- Positioned as an advice column: he describes the newsletter as a weekly answer to reader questions "about building product, driving growth, and accelerating your career". Readers send him questions, so the topics keep coming (excerpt). — [Lenny's Newsletter (self-description)](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/increasing-virality); [On Substack: How Lenny Rachitsky earned $65,000](https://on.substack.com/p/how-lenny-rachitsky-earned-65000)
- Backlog mechanic: "I keep a list of topics I want to write about (have about 50 currently), and have a rough idea of what the next 2-3 posts will be" (excerpt). — [Compound Planning interview](https://manual.compoundplanning.com/chapters/interview-with-lenny-rachitsky-writer-of-lennys-newsletter)
- In his Indie Hackers AMA (around 2021, when the post title said ">$500k ARR") he described a list of about 30 topics that he adds to as reader questions arrive: "I sort them based on what I'm most excited to write about next" (excerpt). — [Indie Hackers AMA](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-write-lennys-newsletter-the-1-paid-business-newsletter-on-substack-generating-over-500k-arr-ama-46fe3dfdc4)
- Workflow (around 2020): each week he picks which questions to answer and creates a separate Coda page per topic to collect notes (excerpt). — [The Proof interview, ~2020](https://theproofwellness.com/interviews/lenny-rachitsky-on-flow-state-ambition-and-angel-investing)
- His process is covered at length in the "How I Write" podcast with David Perell (Oct 2023). I could not get a transcript. — [Goodpods listing](https://goodpods.com/podcasts/how-i-write-274135/lenny-rachitsky-how-this-ex-airbnb-product-manager-created-a-top-newsl-35478473)
- Substack Grow post on his writing habit. — [On Substack](https://on.substack.com/p/how-to-create-consistent-writing-habit-lenny) (could not be fetched)

**Heather Cox Richardson (Letters from an American)**
- Daily input ritual: "I start every morning by reading through social media, a number of different social media sites, probably for an hour to two hours. Just to see what the public conversation is about" (excerpt). — [KALX interview](https://kalx.berkeley.edu/kalx-news-heather-cox-richardson-interview)
- Selection rule: she takes the day's news and places it in a historical framework, focused on the health of American democracy. She calls herself "not a journalist… a historian", and sees her job as interpretation, not reporting what happened. — [Boston College Magazine, "History's First Draft," Fall 2022](https://www.bc.edu/content/bc-web/sites/bc-magazine/fall-2022-issue/features/history-s-first-draft.html); [Wikipedia (secondary)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heather_Cox_Richardson)
- She writes at night and teaches by day. The letters began as late-night Facebook posts before she moved to Substack. — [Boston Globe, Jan 14 2021](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2021/01/14/lifestyle/how-heather-cox-richardson-built-sisterhood-concerned-americans); [Current, Oct 29 2022](https://currentpub.com/2022/10/29/heather-cox-richardson-on-writing-historys-first-draft/)

**Packy McCormick (Not Boring)**
- Every's podcast "How Packy McCormick Finds His Next Big Idea" (Ep. 29, 7 Aug 2024). Show notes list: his thesis on the future of tech (vertically integrated businesses), using AI for thorough research, Claude Projects to edit, making interactive visual graphics, and a Claude-built tool that grades his essays live. — [Chartable episode](https://chartable.com/podcasts/how-do-you-use-chatgpt/episodes/174675971-how-packy-mccormick-finds-his-next-big-idea-ep-29); [Podwise, 7 Aug 2024](https://podwise.ai/episodes/1929945)
- Two quality bars before he publishes: did he create a strong "image"? For company deep-dives, is it the best piece ever written about that company? (paraphrased from show notes) — [How I Write w/ Packy McCormick](https://metacast.app/podcast/how-i-write/RRY6pAWO/packy-mccormick-how-to-publish-your-best-ideas-online/E1uwO8D4)
- Other topics on that episode: technology optimism and bold ideas. — [Good Work with Barrett Brooks](https://chartable.com/podcasts/good-work-with-barrett-brooks/episodes/167545169-technology-optimism-and-embracing-bold-ideas-with-packy-mccormick)

**Ted Gioia (The Honest Broker)**
- He crowdsources topics in yearly "Open Mic" and "asking your advice" posts, noting that "some of the most popular articles here have been quite different from your usual media fare" (excerpt). — [Open Mic at The Honest Broker](https://www.honest-broker.com/p/open-mic-at-the-honest-broker); [I'm Starting the New Year by Asking Your Advice](https://www.honest-broker.com/p/im-starting-the-new-year-by-asking)
- At 18 months, his most-read post was still his origin story, "How I Became the Honest Broker". — [The Honest Broker turns 18 months](https://tedgioia.substack.com/p/the-honest-broker-turns-18-monthshere); [10 Most Popular Posts](https://tedgioia.substack.com/p/the-10-most-popular-posts-on-the)

**Noah Smith (Noahpinion)**
- He polls readers on what they want ("Tell me what you want, what you really really want") and publishes previews of upcoming posts to gauge interest. — [Noahpinion poll post](https://www.noahpinion.blog/p/tell-me-what-you-want-what-you-really); [A preview of upcoming posts](https://www.noahpinion.blog/p/a-preview-of-upcoming-posts)
- His tutorial "How to write a successful Substack" opens by saying advice is "probably overrated", since writers often don't know why what they did worked. — [Noahpinion](https://www.noahpinion.blog/p/how-to-write-a-successful-substack); [Substack Grow Series #16](https://on.substack.com/p/grow-series-16-noah-smith)

**Matt Yglesias (Slow Boring)**
- Mailbag question pipeline (quoted by Brad DeLong; date unclear): a Sunday-evening thread to submit questions, then a Monday-evening thread to vote on three finalists (excerpt). — [Brad DeLong, "Should I Be Running a Mail Answers Column?"](https://braddelong.substack.com/p/should-i-be-running-a-mail-answers)
- Halina Bennet's "The Slow Boring process" (Nov 24 2025, paywalled) goes behind the scenes. The indexed excerpt stops before topic selection. — [Slow Boring](https://www.slowboring.com/p/the-slow-boring-process)

**Emily Oster (ParentData)**
- Mission: translate "the latest scientific research into answers to the questions you have in your daily life". She regularly answers reader questions, including ones from Instagram. When there is no data on a question, she says so. — [ParentData](https://www.parentdata.org/); [Deseret News, Oct 23 2024](https://www.deseret.com/family/2024/10/23/emily-oster-parenting-data-raising-parents-podcast)
- She runs AMA threads on Substack, and ParentData has an AI "librarian" (Petey) that answers from its own archive and her books. — [Substack note](https://substack.com/@emilyoster/note/c-40652477); [ParentData](https://www.parentdata.org/)

**Kyla Scanlon**
- Her topic filter is translating abstract macro (for example, the Fed) into personal impact, because readers want to know how it affects them. She coined "vibecession" in a 2022 Substack post. — [Fortune, Jun 26 2024](https://fortune.com/2024/06/26/kyla-scanlon-tiktok-youtube-newsletter-economist-vibecession); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyla_Scanlon)
- Her stated aim: "my goal is economics education". Social media is just the delivery channel. — [WSJ via WKU PDF, Sep 2024](https://wku.edu/mediarelations/2024/september/sept30/kylascanlon_wsj.pdf)

**Tim Denning**
- Headline-first ideation: he writes headlines first and sends batches of them to himself, and only then writes the article. He frames headlines around the reader's problem, desire or something to avoid. These quotes are secondhand. — [Medium, Illumination's Mirror](https://medium.com/illuminations-mirror/tim-denning-5779d8d52593)
- Recurring themes per a third-party profile: "failure, family, money, mortality, and freedom". — [yespress.io (low reliability)](https://yespress.io/tim-denning)

### Inferences
- Pattern: **backlog plus excitement ranking.** Lenny ranks by "what I'm most excited to write about next". That is a personal-energy filter, not an SEO filter.
- Pattern: **one fixed lens applied to an endless input stream.** HCR (history), Scanlon (personal impact), Oster (data) and Anne Helen Petersen (culture) all turn an unlimited input feed into a coherent brand.
- Pattern: **an audience voting mechanic** (Yglesias's two-step mailbag vote, Noah's polls, Gioia's Open Mic) cuts down the writer's guesswork.
- Packy's method looks thesis-driven: a worldview such as tech optimism or vertical integration produces company deep-dives that test it. I infer this from the show notes; I did not see him describe it as a framework.

### Gaps
- No full transcript of Lenny's or Packy's "How I Write" episodes, so their step-by-step workflows are only partly confirmed.
- I found no primary source for how Ryan Broderick (Garbage Day) sources stories. Background knowledge (unverified): he is a heavy daily scroller of platforms and Reddit, and readers send him links.
- The Free Press's editorial ideation process (pitch meetings, contrarian story selection) is not covered.

## Q2. Niche, positioning, unique angle, and what NOT to write

### Takeaway
The top creators define their niche by **identity plus lens** more than by topic:
- HCR: "historian, not journalist"
- Oster: an economist applying data to parenting
- AHP: "everything is culture"
- Lenny: an ex-Airbnb PM giving practical advice
- Gioia: an arts critic with permission to drift

Explicit "don't write" rules are rarely stated publicly. Mostly they follow from the lens.

### Cited Findings
- HCR positions herself against journalism: she is a historian whose job is to interpret, not to inform about what happened. — [Wikipedia / BC Magazine 2022](https://www.bc.edu/content/bc-web/sites/bc-magazine/fall-2022-issue/features/history-s-first-draft.html)
- Anne Helen Petersen says she can write about anything she wants because everything can be framed as culture, and that she has kept following her interests while still writing about celebrity. — [Seattle Met, May 2025](https://www.seattlemet.com/news-and-city-life/2025/05/anne-helen-petersen-culture-study)
- Ted Gioia set an arts and music focus with admitted "lapses" into topics like burnout or parenting. At launch he "decided to put my total focus on it—turning down other freelance opportunities". — [Jazz Journalists Association, "Gioia goes Substack"](https://news.jazzjournalists.org/gioia-goes-substack/); [Jazz Journal, Apr 23 2021](https://jazzjournal.co.uk/2021/04/23/gioia-promises-honest-jazz-coverage/)
- Slow Boring's stated positioning: "difficult, rigorous conversations that don't always flatter our biases", pragmatism, and a name taken from Max Weber's "strong and slow boring of hard boards". — [Slow Boring About](https://www.slowboring.com/about)
- Noah Smith's tutorial contrasts a "Narrow Substack" (tight focus) with broader formats. The excerpt doesn't define the categories fully. — [Noahpinion tutorial](https://www.noahpinion.blog/p/how-to-write-a-successful-substack)
- Elle Griffin's niche origin story: after querying more than 100 literary agents, she decided her work suited "a niche audience of 1,000" over a mass audience of 100,000. She then serialized her novel with a chapter a week as The Novelleist, and later pivoted to The Elysian, essays on utopian futures, which she calls a "media collective". — [Newsletter Crew, 2022 growth strategy](https://newsletter-crew.pages.dev/blog/switching-it-up-2022-growth-strategy-with-elle-griffin); [The Elysian, "My process"](https://www.elysian.press/i/115849149/my-process)
- Elle Griffin revamped her strategy after year one around what readers found most valuable, and tracks her revenue and subscriber goals publicly. — [Newsletter Crew](https://newsletter-crew.pages.dev/blog/switching-it-up-2022-growth-strategy-with-elle-griffin)
- Oster's "what not to write" heuristic: when no data exists, she says so plainly rather than speculating. — [Deseret News, Oct 2024](https://www.deseret.com/family/2024/10/23/emily-oster-parenting-data-raising-parents-podcast)
- Ryan Broderick hesitated to start a podcast because podcasts are "very tough to grow" and Garbage Day "felt like a big enough job". This is a scope-protection decision. — [Tinkmedia interview](https://tinkmedia.co/interviews/ryan-broderick)

### Inferences
- A positioning formula that fits the evidence: **[credentialed identity] + [single interpretive lens] + [broad input domain]**. A narrow topic is not required. AHP and Gioia both drift on topic but hold their lens.
- Origin-story posts (Gioia's "How I Became the Honest Broker") act as positioning manifestos and can stay the top post for years.
- "Not writing" mostly takes the form of declining adjacent formats or channels (Broderick on podcasts, Gioia on freelance work), not topic bans.

### Gaps
- I found no public "what I won't write about" list from any of the named creators.
- No Lyz Lenz positioning source (Men Yell at Me) turned up. A forum post claimed she was leaving Substack; that is unverified.

## Q3. Recurring post formats and series, and why

### Takeaway
Most top writers run a **high-effort flagship** (deep dive, essay or column) alongside **low-effort, community-driven recurring slots** (mailbags, threads, Q&As, roundups). The recurring slots keep up cadence and engagement, and often serve as paid perks.

### Cited Findings
- Lenny: a Q&A, advice-column format answering weekly reader questions. — [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/increasing-virality)
- Slow Boring: four columns a week (sometimes more), a mailbag column on Fridays per the About page, and occasional interview podcasts. A November 2025 post shows the mailbag moved to Wednesday for a holiday, so the regular day is unclear. — [Slow Boring About](https://www.slowboring.com/about); [Artisanal human-crafted mailbag](https://www.slowboring.com/p/artisanal-human-crafted-mailbag)
- Culture Study: two newsletters a week almost every week, plus subscriber-only "Threads" twice a week (such as "Tuesday Thread: what are you watching") and a podcast 50 weeks a year. — [Culture Study, Tuesday Thread](https://annehelen.substack.com/p/tuesday-thread-what-are-you-watching-f42); [Culture Study home](https://annehelen.substack.com/)
- Garbage Day is described as a "thrice-weekly newsletter" with over 100,000 subscribers chronicling internet culture (2024 profile). — [The Story (AU) profiles](https://thestory.au/categories/profiles)
- ParentData: two weekly posts (free, per an older page), AMA threads, a podcast, and spin-off verticals such as Hot Flash (women's health). — [ParentData join page](https://parentdata.org/join-today/); [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/parentdata-by-emily-oster/id1633515294)
- Not Boring: deep company essays, some sponsored "deep dives", plus interactive graphics (per Aug 2024 show notes). — [Chartable Ep. 29](https://chartable.com/podcasts/how-do-you-use-chatgpt/episodes/174675971-how-packy-mccormick-finds-his-next-big-idea-ep-29)
- Gioia: annual reader "Open Mic" posts, top-10 retrospectives, and milestone posts. — [Open Mic](https://tedgioia.substack.com/p/open-mic-at-the-honest-broker); [10 Most Popular](https://tedgioia.substack.com/p/the-10-most-popular-posts-on-the)
- Noah Smith: he publishes interviews (for example, with Matt Yglesias) and says free posts are "much better marketing than paid posts". He keeps most posts free. — [Noahpinion interview](https://www.noahpinion.blog/p/interview-matt-yglesias-author-and); [Substack Grow #16](https://on.substack.com/p/grow-series-16-noah-smith)
- Substack's own "Grow" guidance on advice columns as a format. — [On Substack: Advice on advice columns](https://on.substack.com/p/advice-on-advice-columns)

### Inferences
- Threads and mailbags are ideation engines too: the questions in them become future flagship topics (clear in Lenny's case, likely for Yglesias).
- Paid-only community formats (AHP's Threads) let a writer paywall community instead of the flagship writing, which matches Noah's "free is marketing" stance.
- [unverified, from background knowledge] Packy runs a "Weekly Dose of Optimism" roundup alongside his essays, and Lenny moved much of his output to a podcast plus guest posts. Neither is confirmed in this session.

### Gaps
- No primary rationale from these creators for why they chose specific formats, beyond Noah's free/paid marketing logic.

## Q4. Publishing cadence and how it shapes ideation

### Takeaway
Cadence runs from daily (HCR) to weekly or less (Lenny, and Packy from 2025). Daily cadence pushes a writer toward news-pegged interpretation; weekly or slower pushes toward a backlog and deep research. Packy explicitly moved toward fewer, deeper pieces.

### Cited Findings
- HCR: nightly letters written after a day of teaching, fed by a 1–2 hour morning social-media scan. — [KALX](https://kalx.berkeley.edu/kalx-news-heather-cox-richardson-interview); [Boston Globe 2021](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2021/01/14/lifestyle/how-heather-cox-richardson-built-sisterhood-concerned-americans)
- Lenny: a weekly cadence, with a plan for the next 2–3 posts drawn from a 30–50 topic backlog. — [Compound Planning](https://manual.compoundplanning.com/chapters/interview-with-lenny-rachitsky-writer-of-lennys-newsletter); [Indie Hackers AMA](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-write-lennys-newsletter-the-1-paid-business-newsletter-on-substack-generating-over-500k-arr-ama-46fe3dfdc4)
- Packy: early on he would write "a paragraph or two" before sleep (around 10:30–11pm) if he hadn't hit his daily goal. From late summer 2020 his essays got "more serious, longer, and weedier". In 2025 he shifted from high-frequency posts to fewer, deeper, heavily researched essays (per secondary summaries). — [The Proof, Feb 2021](https://theproofwellness.com/packy-mccormick-on-writing-flow-finding-his-voice); [Aakash Gupta, "Packy McCormick's Story"](https://www.news.aakashg.com/p/packy-mccormicks-story); [Case-study summary, Dec 23 2025](https://product-management-case-studies.vercel.app/case-studies/my-5-favorite-not-boring-essays-of-2025)
- Yglesias: "a meaty post… almost every weekday" (2022). — [Full Stack Economics, 2022](https://www.fullstackeconomics.com/p/six-economics-related-newsletters-you-should-read-in-2022)
- Noah Smith: around five posts a week, three free and two paywalled (third-party). He argues email matters more than web traffic, because without email "traffic will be extremely volatile". — [Everything-PR](https://everything-pr.com/noah-smith-noahpinion-substack); [Noahpinion tutorial](https://www.noahpinion.blog/p/how-to-write-a-successful-substack)
- AHP: two posts plus two Threads a week, and a podcast 50 weeks a year. — [Culture Study](https://annehelen.substack.com/)
- Garbage Day: three times a week (2024). — [The Story](https://thestory.au/categories/profiles)

### Inferences
- High-frequency writers (HCR, Noah, Yglesias, Broderick) depend on the news cycle as their idea supply. Weekly writers (Lenny, Packy) depend on a backlog or thesis.
- Low-effort recurring slots let high-frequency writers keep cadence without a new big idea every day.

### Gaps
- No verified 2026 cadence data for any creator.

## Q5. Publicly shared frameworks

### Takeaway
Few creators publish formal frameworks. The closest are Lenny's question backlog ranked by excitement, Yglesias's submit-then-vote mailbag, Packy's pre-publish quality bars plus an AI essay grader, Denning's headline-first batching, and Noah's "free signups are the key metric" growth doctrine.

### Cited Findings
- Lenny: reader question → topic list (30–50) → ranked by excitement → plan the next 2–3 posts → one Coda page per topic. — [Indie Hackers AMA](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-write-lennys-newsletter-the-1-paid-business-newsletter-on-substack-generating-over-500k-arr-ama-46fe3dfdc4); [The Proof](https://theproofwellness.com/interviews/lenny-rachitsky-on-flow-state-ambition-and-angel-investing)
- Yglesias: a Sunday submit thread, then a Monday vote on three finalists. — [DeLong](https://braddelong.substack.com/p/should-i-be-running-a-mail-answers)
- Packy: a strong "image", "best piece ever written about that company", and a Claude Projects essay grader (Aug 2024). — [How I Write](https://metacast.app/podcast/how-i-write/RRY6pAWO/packy-mccormick-how-to-publish-your-best-ideas-online/E1uwO8D4); [Chartable](https://chartable.com/podcasts/how-do-you-use-chatgpt/episodes/174675971-how-packy-mccormick-finds-his-next-big-idea-ep-29)
- Tim Denning: write headlines first, batch them to yourself, and make the headline about the reader (secondhand). — [Medium](https://medium.com/illuminations-mirror/tim-denning-5779d8d52593)
- Noah Smith: "free email list signups are the most important metric"; free posts are better marketing. — [Noahpinion tutorial](https://www.noahpinion.blog/p/how-to-write-a-successful-substack); [Grow #16](https://on.substack.com/p/grow-series-16-noah-smith)
- HCR: a morning public-conversation scan → pick what matters for democracy → explain it with historical context, written that night. — [KALX](https://kalx.berkeley.edu/kalx-news-heather-cox-richardson-interview)

### Inferences
- These could be turned into agent-ready routines: (a) an ingest-and-rank backlog, (b) a daily news scan through a fixed lens, (c) a reader-voting slot, (d) headline-first batching, and (e) a pre-publish rubric (Packy-style AI grader).

### Gaps
- Not verified: Paul Krugman's Substack process (since leaving NYT in late 2024), The Free Press's editorial process, Garbage Day's sourcing, and Lyz Lenz's niche statements. No primary sources were found within the search budget.
- Full texts of Substack's Grow series (Lenny, Noah) and Noah's tutorial need a fetch from an allowlisted network to confirm exact wording.
