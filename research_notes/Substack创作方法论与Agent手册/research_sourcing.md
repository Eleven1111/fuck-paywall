# How Top Substack Creators Gather Source Material (Research / Material Pipeline)

Method note: compiled 2026-11-03. WebFetch could not reach any target domain in this session (DNS blocked: lennysnewsletter.com, bc.edu, substack subdomains), so **all findings below come from web-search result snippets/summaries, not from full-page reads**. Quotes marked "quoted" were rendered as quotes in search snippets; they should be spot-checked against the original page before publication. Dates are given where known; "date unknown" means the snippet did not show one.

## 1. Information diets, feeds and tools (RSS, X lists, read-later, Notion/Obsidian, Readwise)

### Takeaway
The most concrete, documented tool stacks are Ryan Broderick (Raindrop bookmark folders -> Google Doc -> morning triage) and Casey Newton (Readwise, Capacities, Raycast, NotebookLM, Notion; plus a scheduled Claude "sweep"). Most other top writers describe their diet as "read a lot" (books, papers, news, social) rather than naming tools. Social media itself is a primary monitoring feed for HCR, Broderick, Tooze and Lenny.

### Cited Findings
- **Ryan Broderick (Garbage Day):** uses a "passive planning system" — saves links throughout the day into folders in **Raindrop** (synced across devices), with separate folders for the newsletter, live shows and podcast ideas. — [The Story, "Inbox Icons"](https://thestory.au/articles/ryan-broderick-garbage-day-newsletter-interview/) (date not shown in snippet; c. 2024–25)
- Broderick on writing mornings empties a running **Google Doc** of collected items into a browser and checks each link for deletion/updates before assembling the issue; says he has "a few systems for tracking stories and content across the week" that let him make connections. — [The Story](https://thestory.au/articles/ryan-broderick-garbage-day-newsletter-interview/)
- Broderick says he no longer trawls 4chan: "I'm not going and trolling around 4chan anymore for stories." — [Depth Perception (Long Lead)](https://depthperception.longlead.com/p/ryan-broderick-garbage-day-newsletter-panic-world-podcast-internet-culture)
- Broderick earlier said he set up Reddit and Tumblr accounts to keep up with what's happening online (older interview). — [Follow Friday podcast](https://followfridaypodcast.com/ryan-broderick)
- **Heather Cox Richardson:** starts each day reading social media "for a couple of hours just to see what people are talking about and what the stories are." — [The Tyee, Apr 4 2025](https://thetyee.ca/Culture/2025/04/04/Hope-America-Heather-Cox-Richardson/)
- **Casey Newton (Platformer):** wrote a productivity piece on "three apps that made me more productive this year" — **Capacities, Raycast, Readwise** (per URL slug); describes **NotebookLM** as letting you "save a bunch of PDFs into virtual notebooks and ask questions of them collectively." — [Platformer](https://www.platformer.news/ai-productivity-apps-capacities-raycast-readwise/) (c. 2024–25)
- Newton (Aug 2023) wrote that "three years of using expensive note-taking software doesn't seem to be making me a better thinker," examining whether AI tools can help; also said he expects to talk to his Notion database "as if it's ChatGPT." — [Casey Newton on X](https://twitter.com/CaseyNewton/status/1694870152276570328); [Platformer post comments](https://platformer.substack.com/p/why-note-taking-apps-dont-make-us/comments)
- **Ted Gioia (The Honest Broker):** starts every day reading books for ~two hours and reads more at night; elsewhere says reading "at least three hours per day"; sets alarm an hour or so early so he can read first. — [I Answer 18 Questions](https://www.honest-broker.com/p/i-answer-18-questions); [My Lifetime Reading Plan](https://www.honest-broker.com/p/my-lifetime-reading-plan); 2+ hour video interview on routine: [How I Write](https://www.honest-broker.com/p/how-i-write)
- Gioia's "most emphatic" advice is reported as "read more than you write"; reading is "like fuel in the tank" (secondhand paraphrase). — [Athens blog summary](https://tryathens.com/blog/ted-gioia-writing-advice)
- **Kyla Scanlon:** "for the daily process, a lot of it is just reading a lot"; routine described as waking early, reading news first, working out to think, producing video ~3pm, writing at night. — [Mercatus Macro Musings](https://www.mercatus.org/macro-musings/kyla-scanlon-vibecession-vibe-economy-and-path-growing-american-wealth); [Barry Ritholtz transcript, Oct 2024](https://ritholtz.com/2024/10/transcript-kyla-scanlon/)
- Claim that Scanlon spends "40% of her day reading" comes only from a secondary blog — unverified. — [Antoine Buteau, Lessons from Kyla Scanlon](https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-kyla-scanlon/)
- **Noah Smith:** advocates heavy reading before writing — cites a PhD advisor's rule of ~100 papers read per paper written, suggesting ~20 for blog posts. — [Noah Smith's writing advice](https://www.noahpinion.blog/p/noah-smiths-writing-advice)
- **Adam Tooze (Chartbook):** began (Nov 2020) as an experiment collecting "significant images, charts and links that I tweeted out over the last few days" — i.e., his Twitter/X feed was the capture layer; paid "Top Links" emails collect "the most interesting, engaging or simply weird links of the previous few days." — [Chartbook #1](https://adamtooze.com/2020/11/15/chartbook-1/); [Chartbook About](https://adamtooze.substack.com/about); example [Top Links 1272](https://adamtooze.substack.com/p/top-links-1272-the-shekel-caught)
- **Lenny Rachitsky:** can "tweet out a question and get a ton of different perspectives super quickly" (X as crowdsourcing feed). — [Compound Planning interview](https://manual.compoundplanning.com/chapters/interview-with-lenny-rachitsky-writer-of-lennys-newsletter) (older, c. 2021)

### Inferences
- Common pattern: a low-friction **capture inbox** (bookmarks, tweets, notes app) feeding a **periodic triage** step right before writing. Broderick's Raindrop->Doc->triage loop is the most replicable documented example.
- Link-roundup formats (Tooze Top Links, Garbage Day) are essentially "publish the capture inbox, curated."

### Gaps
- No sources found on RSS reader use, specific X lists, or Obsidian for any named creator.
- No documented tool stack found for HCR, Tooze, Noah Smith, Gioia, Doomberg, Scanlon, Oster.
- Could not open Newton's productivity post to confirm exactly how he uses Readwise/Capacities in reporting.

## 2. Capturing and organizing notes / idea snippets ("swipe file", second brain)

### Takeaway
Top writers mostly use simple systems: running topic lists and messy research docs (Lenny), a simple idea-note list (Orosz), notes app (Scanlon), bookmark folders (Broderick). Newton is the explicit skeptic of elaborate second-brain software. Doomberg uses a title-first, one-sentence-per-paragraph outline.

### Cited Findings
- **Lenny:** keeps a running list of ~50 topics with a rough idea of the next 2–3 posts; for research posts, "throws all of his findings into a doc, in a messy way," keeps adding, then molds "raw nuggets" into a post. — [Compound Planning interview](https://manual.compoundplanning.com/chapters/interview-with-lenny-rachitsky-writer-of-lennys-newsletter); also [David Perell "How I Write" episode](https://goodpods.com/podcasts/how-i-write-274135/lenny-rachitsky-how-this-ex-airbnb-product-manager-created-a-top-newsl-35478473) (c. Oct 2023)
- **Gergely Orosz:** takes notes whenever a writable idea comes up and reviews them when he feels like writing; splits work across days for research, drafts, feedback from trusted people and editing with an editor; works each deep-dive over multiple weeks to keep ~two articles ready per week; notes that too many drafts in progress get overwhelming since some go stale before research finishes. — [Lenny's Podcast summary, Aug 1 2023](https://podpage.com/lennys-podcast/blog/summary-leaving-big-tech-to-build-the-1-technology-newsletter-gergely-orosz-the-pragmatic-engin); [Antoine Buteau, Lessons from Gergely Orosz](https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-gergely-orosz/)
- Orosz wrote parts of *The Software Engineer's Guidebook* as newsletter posts to get feedback. — [Guidebook recap, Nov 2025](https://thepixelpioneers.blogspot.com/2025/11/the-software-engineers-guidebook-recap.html)
- **Kyla Scanlon:** writes video scripts in her notes app; says she's "kind of writing her newsletter throughout the week"; "I write how I think"; draws her own visuals to synthesize. — [Ritholtz transcript, Oct 2024](https://ritholtz.com/2024/10/transcript-kyla-scanlon/); [Growth In Reverse](https://growthinreverse.com/kyla-scanlon/)
- **Doomberg:** starts with "an intuitive sense of what it is that I want to say," researches around that core; picks a title first, then "write a one sentence summary of each of the paragraphs." — [Fire2Fission podcast ep. 50](https://www.fire2fission.com/podcast-episodes/050-doomberg)
- **Noah Smith:** "doesn't have much of a formal process" — sometimes writes section headings first; rarely edits. — [Noah Smith's writing advice](https://www.noahpinion.blog/p/noah-smiths-writing-advice)
- **Casey Newton:** skeptical that note-taking software makes him a better thinker (2023). — [X post](https://twitter.com/CaseyNewton/status/1694870152276570328)
- **Packy McCormick:** a 2025 piece says he recorded an idea into Claude, asked for feedback and "chatted the whole way" (voice/AI as capture). — [search summary of Not Boring essays incl. Most Human Wins](https://www.notboring.co/p/most-human-wins) (exact essay attribution unconfirmed)

### Inferences
- Writers at the top of Substack rely more on volume of reading + a "messy doc per post" than on Zettelkasten-style systems. A minimal replicable kit: topic backlog list + per-post research dump doc + bookmark inbox.

### Gaps
- No creator found describing an explicit "swipe file" of others' writing.
- No confirmed Obsidian/Roam use by any named creator.

## 3. Primary sources, data, audience surveys, expert interviews, community input

### Takeaway
The strongest repeatable research engines are (a) large **reader surveys** analyzed by a dedicated data person (Lenny + Noam Segal; Orosz + Elin Nilsson), (b) **insider/expert sourcing** (Orosz, Newton), (c) **primary documents/public data** (HCR; Noah Smith with FRED; Tooze's charts; Oster's literature reviews), and (d) **co-authored essays with domain experts** (Packy's Not Boring World).

### Cited Findings
**Surveys**
- Lenny's Newsletter AI productivity survey (~1,750 PMs, engineers, designers, founders; Dec 2025); "over 70% of PMs and founders, and 60% of designers" reported AI improved work quality; only 51% of engineers said quality improved, 21% worse. Lenny credited **Noam Segal** (AI Insights, Figma) with building, running and analyzing the survey. — [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/ai-tools-are-overdelivering-results); [Lenny on X](https://x.com/lennysan/status/2009308394918707476)
- Lenny tool-stack survey of ~6,500 tech professionals (2025): 17% use Cursor regularly; >50% combine AI assistants (e.g., ChatGPT + Perplexity for deep research). — [What's in your stack 2025](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/whats-in-your-stack-the-state-of)
- Annual Tech Worker Sentiment Survey (2026, second year), with Segal. — [How tech workers actually feel about AI in 2026](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-tech-workers-actually-feel-about)
- Pragmatic Engineer 2025 tech-stack survey: 3,000+ responses, report co-credited to Orosz and **Elin Nilsson**; "Thanks to Elin, we can take on ambitious projects." — [2025 Survey Part 1](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/the-pragmatic-engineer-2025-survey); [Survey invite](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/survey-whats-in-your-tech-stack); [One million readers](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/one-million)
- Orosz's State of Frontend 2022 analysis: 3,700 respondents, 125 countries. — [State of Frontend 2022](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/state-of-frontend-2022)
- First survey of PE's own reader base in 2024. — [Wrapped 2024](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/wrapped-the-pragmatic-engineer-in)

**Expert/insider interviews**
- Orosz: most pieces now rest on "research, interviews, or analysis of events" rather than personal experience; was hiring a tech-industry analyst to help with research (~year four). — [Lenny's Podcast summary, Aug 2023](https://podpage.com/lennys-podcast/blog/summary-leaving-big-tech-to-build-the-1-technology-newsletter-gergely-orosz-the-pragmatic-engin); [Buteau](https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-gergely-orosz/)
- Platformer: Casey "speaks regularly with company leaders, current and former employees, policymakers, academics, and other experts"; quotes sources by name whenever possible; describes anonymous sources' roles as specifically as possible. — [Platformer About](https://www.platformer.news/about/)
- Newton (Apr 2026) is revamping Platformer toward **more scoops, less aggregation/analysis** explicitly "to compete with AI." — [Nieman Lab, Apr 2026](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/04/more-scoops-less-aggregation-and-analysis-how-casey-newton-is-revamping-his-newsletter-to-compete-with-ai/); [Platformer: How we're shaking up Platformer for the AI era](https://www.platformer.news/platformer-schedule-changes-ai-automation/)
- Kyla Scanlon runs interviews in the newsletter, e.g., with Lael Brainard (2026). — [Lael Brainard on Why Groceries Cost So Much](https://kyla.substack.com/p/lael-brainard-on-why-groceries-cost); she also runs a reader Q&A intake: [Ask Kyla](https://ask.kylascanlon.com/)
- Kyla pays attention to feedback from investors, "what resonates with people and where gaps seem to be." — [Growth In Reverse](https://growthinreverse.com/kyla-scanlon/)

**Primary sources & data**
- HCR anchors analysis in primary sources ("the actual letters, the actual speeches, the actual records") — this phrasing came from a low-quality page; treat cautiously. Boston College Magazine (Fall 2022) covers how she picks stories. — [BC Magazine, "History's First Draft," Fall 2022](https://www.bc.edu/content/bc-web/sites/bc-magazine/fall-2022-issue/features/history-s-first-draft.html); [Current Publishing, Oct 2022](https://currentpub.com/2022/10/29/heather-cox-richardson-on-writing-historys-first-draft/)
- Noah Smith checks viral claims directly against **FRED** ("Let's go to FRED, the data page maintained by the Federal Reserve"), e.g., indexing median household income and rent CPI to 1985. — [How not to be fooled by viral charts](https://www.noahpinion.blog/p/how-not-to-be-fooled-by-viral-charts)
- Emily Oster / ParentData: says it reviews "hundreds of studies" per topic via Google Scholar, PubMed and medical databases; favors most-cited studies from respected journals, randomized data, large samples, objective measures; promises clear methodology and honesty about limitations. Team includes OB-GYN Nathan Fox, MD. — [ParentData About](https://parentdata.org/about-us/); [ParentData plans page](https://parentdata.org/plans/parentdata/); [ParentData home](https://parentdata.org/)
- Doomberg: team describes itself as former commodity-sector executives/consultants; one member a scientist who led research teams, the editor-in-chief with a finance background; core analytic lens: is the world in "an abundance of primary energy or a shortage"; uses a "truth assessment model" for viral science claims. — [Fire2Fission ep. 50](https://www.fire2fission.com/podcast-episodes/050-doomberg); [Motley Fool interview, Oct 2022](https://www.fool.com/investing/2022/10/08/doombergs-head-writer-on-europes-energy-crisis-and/); [Interview with Doomberg (Substack)](https://substack.com/home/post/p-146028119)

**Collaborators / co-authors as sources**
- Packy's "Not Boring World" (Jan 2026) features co-written essays where co-authors trade domain expertise for his writing and distribution (256K+ subscribers); his two most popular essays were co-written with founders. — third-party summary: [hq-raydata summary of "Welcome to Not Boring World"](https://hq-raydata-co.pages.dev/vault/06-reference/2026-01-06-not-boring-welcome-to-not-boring-world/) (verify on notboring.co)

**Community input**
- Lenny's newsletter began as an advice column: readers send questions and he answers them. — [Compound Planning](https://manual.compoundplanning.com/chapters/interview-with-lenny-rachitsky-writer-of-lennys-newsletter)
- ParentData uses "Petey," an AI librarian, to answer reader questions; unanswered ones go to the team. — [ParentData](https://parentdata.org/)

### Inferences
- Surveys + a named analyst partner turn audience size into proprietary data — a moat that aggregation (and AI) can't copy. Newton's 2026 pivot to scoops reflects the same logic.
- Expert co-authorship (Packy) and guest posts are a scalable sourcing model for generalists.

### Gaps
- No sources found on how any of these creators use Substack Chat or comments as research input specifically.
- Could not confirm HCR's specific primary-source list (e.g., Congressional Record, court filings) — BC Magazine article unreadable here.
- Tooze's reading/data workflow undocumented in found sources.

## 4. Fact-checking, citations, accuracy

### Takeaway
Explicit accuracy rules are documented for HCR (three-source corroboration), Platformer (named sourcing policy), ParentData (stated study-quality criteria), Orosz (trusted-reviewer feedback + editor), and Noah Smith (go back to official data). Formal fact-checkers are not documented for any of them.

### Cited Findings
- HCR: does not run anything unless corroborated by **three different sources**; once slowed writing to confirm the location of a US Navy aircraft carrier. — [Brian Boyles, "My conversation with Heather Cox Richardson"](https://brianboyles.substack.com/p/my-conversation-with-heather-cox) (via search snippet)
- Platformer: named sources wherever possible; specific role descriptions for anonymous sources. — [Platformer About](https://www.platformer.news/about/)
- Orosz: drafts go to trusted people for feedback, then editing with an editor. — [Lenny's Podcast summary](https://podpage.com/lennys-podcast/blog/summary-leaving-big-tech-to-build-the-1-technology-newsletter-gergely-orosz-the-pragmatic-engin)
- Orosz: "doing numbers" has "never been a goal" — survey data is self-selected reader samples, not representative. — [PE Three Years](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/the-pragmatic-engineer-three-years)
- Noah Smith: debunks viral charts by rebuilding them from FRED source series. — [How not to be fooled by viral charts](https://www.noahpinion.blog/p/how-not-to-be-fooled-by-viral-charts)
- ParentData: methodology transparency and stated limitations as an explicit commitment. — [ParentData plans](https://parentdata.org/plans/parentdata/)
- Doomberg: anonymity so critics "attack your ideas, as opposed to attacking the person"; "Our writing and research should be viewed independently of the authors." — [Fire2Fission](https://www.fire2fission.com/podcast-episodes/050-doomberg)
- Tooze: Chartbook #1 notes all images are linked to their sources. — [Chartbook #1](https://adamtooze.com/2020/11/15/chartbook-1/)
- Broderick: re-checks every saved link for deletion/updates before publishing. — [The Story](https://thestory.au/articles/ryan-broderick-garbage-day-newsletter-interview/)

### Inferences
- Solo-newsletter accuracy is mostly a personal rule set (N-source rule, link-back-to-source, recheck before send) rather than an institutional desk.

### Gaps
- No public correction policies found for these newsletters; no evidence of paid fact-checkers.

## 5. Researchers, editors, and AI tools in sourcing

### Takeaway
Hired/partner researchers are documented for Orosz (Elin Nilsson) and Lenny (Noam Segal for surveys); Orosz uses an editor. AI use is openly discussed by Packy (Claude as editor, research help, derivative artifacts), Newton (Claude weekly sweep — secondhand; NotebookLM; 2026 restructuring for the "AI era"), and ParentData (AI librarian for reader Qs).

### Cited Findings
- Orosz hired a tech-industry analyst/researcher; Elin Nilsson co-credited on surveys/deep dives. — [Buteau](https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-gergely-orosz/); [PE 2025 Survey](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/the-pragmatic-engineer-2025-survey); [One million](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/one-million)
- Lenny's surveys built/run/analyzed by Noam Segal. — [Lenny on X](https://x.com/lennysan/status/2009308394918707476)
- Packy: "I've been using Claude as an editor for the past few months. It's great, but it gives me an A- on practically any first draft I send it." (Aug 2024) — [Packy on X](https://x.com/packyM/status/1821163105830769100); Every "AI and I" ep. 29 with Dan Shipper: using AI to understand dense concepts, be thorough in research, Claude Projects to edit, and a live-built essay-grading tool. — [Every](https://every.to/chain-of-thought/how-packy-mccormick-finds-his-next-big-idea); [Dan Shipper on X](https://x.com/danshipper/status/1821161412367884367)
- Packy essays credit "my AI editor, Claude Opus" and ChatGPT/Claude for research and editing; on a robotics essay he had Claude generate a game, a full annotated bibliography and a use-case slider "in a couple of prompts." — [Substack note](https://substack.com/@notboring/note/c-200715164); [The Goldilocks Zone](https://www.notboring.co/p/the-goldilocks-zone) (specific credit lines per essay not individually verified)
- Packy research process discussed in Macro Ops podcast (show notes only). — [Macro Ops](https://macro-ops.com/?p=15248)
- Casey Newton: a third-party newsletter reports a scheduled Thursday Claude process that "scours multiple spaces"; "The AI does the sweep, then I do the edit." **Secondhand — unverified** whether this quote is Newton's. — [Project C newsletter](https://newsletter.projectc.biz/p/agreeing-to-disagree-with-casey-newton-on-ai)
- Newton's 2026 restructuring explicitly frames schedule changes around AI/automation and more original reporting. — [Platformer](https://www.platformer.news/platformer-schedule-changes-ai-automation/); [Nieman Lab Apr 2026](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/04/more-scoops-less-aggregation-and-analysis-how-casey-newton-is-revamping-his-newsletter-to-compete-with-ai/)
- ParentData's "Petey" AI librarian. — [ParentData](https://parentdata.org/)
- Lenny's reader survey found >50% combine AI assistants, e.g., ChatGPT + Perplexity for deep research (about readers, not Lenny himself). — [Lenny's stack survey](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/whats-in-your-stack-the-state-of)

### Inferences
- Emerging division of labor: AI for sweep/summarize/edit/derivative assets; humans for sourcing, judgment, scoops. Newton's pivot and Orosz's researcher hire both point to proprietary reporting/data as the defensible layer.

### Gaps
- No statements found on AI use by HCR, Tooze, Noah Smith, Gioia, Doomberg, Scanlon, Lenny (personally), or Orosz (personally).
- HCR's team (e.g., any research help) not documented in found sources.
- Full text of Newton's Apr 2026 post and Nieman Lab article not read; specifics of his AI workflow unconfirmed.
