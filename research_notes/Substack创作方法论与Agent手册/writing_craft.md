# Writing Craft and Drafting/Editing Workflows of Top Substack Creators

> Research notes compiled 2026-11-03. **Method caveat:** WebSearch worked, but WebFetch could not reach most primary hosts (on.substack.com, *.substack.com, noahpinion.blog, niemanlab.org and bc.edu all failed DNS in this sandbox). Most findings below therefore come from search-result snippets and summaries of the cited pages, not from full-text reads. Quotes marked "(snippet)" are as the search engine summarized them and should be spot-checked against the URL before anyone quotes them verbatim. Items from my background knowledge that I could not verify are listed only under Inferences or Gaps and are labeled as such.

## 1. Post structures and templates

### Takeaway
The top creators use a few repeatable "containers": the daily historical letter (Richardson), the advice-column or answer-a-question deep dive (Rachitsky), the long researched company or tech essay (McCormick, Orosz), and a links section plus a column (Newton). Each format is tied to a fixed cadence. Several of them changed format between 2020 and 2026, moving toward fewer, deeper pieces or toward original reporting.

### Cited Findings
- **Lenny Rachitsky (Lenny's Newsletter):** He describes the newsletter as an advice column: readers send questions and he answers them as well as he can. He keeps a list of topics and has a rough idea of the next 2–3 posts. — [Compound Planning interview](https://manual.compoundplanning.com/chapters/interview-with-lenny-rachitsky-writer-of-lennys-newsletter)
- Rachitsky is described as balancing storytelling with practicality and creating "high-signal" content (episode description; transcript not read). — [How I Write w/ David Perell, Oct 2023 (Goodpods listing)](https://goodpods.com/podcasts/how-i-write-274135/lenny-rachitsky-how-this-ex-airbnb-product-manager-created-a-top-newsl-35478473)
- A podcast chapter list attributes to him the advice to "visualize the idea in the simplest way possible on paper," with a focus on clarity. — [Lenny Rachitsky, 1M-subscriber episode summary (2025/26)](https://podcasts-distilled-hugo-content.pages.dev/lennys-podcast-product-career-growth/how-i-built-a-1m-subscriber-newsletter-and-top-10-tech-podcast-lenny-rachitsky/)
- **Heather Cox Richardson (Letters from an American):** The letter grew out of Facebook posts and moved to Substack, where it is published almost daily. Topic choice follows the public conversation: she reads social media for 1–2 hours to see what people are discussing, then "just write[s] what I found" (snippet). — [Boston College Magazine, Fall 2022 "History's First Draft"](https://www.bc.edu/content/bc-web/sites/bc-magazine/fall-2022-issue/features/history-s-first-draft.html); [Current Publishing, 2022-10-29](https://currentpub.com/2022/10/29/heather-cox-richardson-on-writing-historys-first-draft/); [Bill Moyers interview](https://billmoyers.com/story/bill-moyers-and-heather-cox-richards-on-her-daily-letters/)
- **Packy McCormick (Not Boring):** Around late summer 2020 his essays became "a little bit more serious, a little bit longer, and a little bit weedsier" (snippet). A 2025 retrospective describes a strategic shift from high-frequency publishing to fewer, deeper essays that needed extensive research. His stated bar for company pieces is to write "the best piece that's ever been written about this company" (snippet). — [Aakash Gupta, "Packy McCormick's Story"](https://www.news.aakashg.com/p/packy-mccormicks-story); [Not Boring one-year retrospective mirror](https://hq-raydata-co.pages.dev/vault/06-reference/2026-04-03-not-boring-one-year-retrospective/); [Value Hive podcast ep. 55](https://macro-ops.com/?p=15248)
- **Gergely Orosz (The Pragmatic Engineer):** Two issues a week: an in-depth deep dive on Tuesday and a timely news or scoop issue on Thursday. Most pieces are "based on research, interviews or analyzing events" (snippet). — [Gergely transcript, Lenny's Podcast (via lenny-rag)](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/@mpnikhil/lenny-rag-mcp/blob/b181b8f2cf8a242f98c80f43cae5ce42514d3771/preprocessed/Gergely.json); [Write That Blog interview](https://writethatblog.substack.com/p/gergely-orosz-on-technical-blogging)
- **Casey Newton (Platformer):** The issue combines a links section, which Newton and Zoë Schiffer worked on together, with a column that Newton writes. Some editions are "essentially just a straight ahead news story." A 2020 profile says it went out daily at 5 p.m. — [Voices.media transcript](https://voices.media/transcript-casey-newton-founder-platformer/); [Talking Biz News](https://talkingbiznews.com/media-news/why-tech-journalist-newton-launched-a-newsletter/)
- In April 2026 Nieman Lab reported that Newton was revamping Platformer toward "more scoops, less aggregation and analysis" to compete with AI. — [Nieman Lab, Apr 2026](https://niemanlab.org/2026/04/more-scoops-less-aggregation-and-analysis-how-casey-newton-is-revamping-his-newsletter-to-compete-with-ai) (headline only; full text not fetched)
- **Matt Yglesias (Slow Boring):** It describes itself as "a daily newsletter… about politics and public policy," and Yglesias has written online "day in, day out" for about 21 years. Slow Boring has process posts ("The Slow Boring process" and "Why I write such excellent posts"), but both are paywalled. — [Slow Boring About](https://www.slowboring.com/about); [Max Read, "Matt Yglesias and the secret of blogging"](https://maxread.substack.com/p/matt-yglesias-and-the-secret-of-blogging); [The Slow Boring process](https://www.slowboring.com/p/the-slow-boring-process)
- **Anne Helen Petersen (Culture Study):** She started it in 2016 as "The Collected AHP," partly because she missed the loose, tangent-filled style of early blogging. She chose "Culture Study" because the term is expansive and anything can be framed as culture. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Helen_Petersen); [Culture Study About](https://annehelen.substack.com/about); [The Tilt profile](https://www.thetilt.com/content-entrepreneur/anne-helen-petersen-content-business)
- **Elle Griffin (The Elysian):** She publishes essay series and collections. Paid subscribers get every collection online, and "Collectors" get print pamphlets of each series, about 4–6 a year. — [The Elysian](https://www.elysian.press/p/are-newsletter-subscriptions-over); [The Elysian "My process" section](https://www.elysian.press/i/115849149/my-process) (section not fetched)

### Inferences
- Format follows the source of value. Richardson's value is speed and context, so she publishes daily. Orosz, McCormick and Rachitsky sell depth, so they publish fewer pieces and research each one heavily. Newton's 2026 shift suggests that aggregation and commentary formats are under pressure from AI summarization (inference from the Nieman headline).
- Unverified, from background knowledge: Lenny's posts typically open with a greeting and a one-line summary of the reader question, followed by numbered sections or frameworks. Orosz deep dives typically open with a bulleted "In this issue" list. Neither was confirmed in this session.

### Gaps
- I found no verbatim templates, such as TL;DR blocks or section headers, quoted from primary sources, because the post pages could not be fetched.
- I found nothing usable on Kyla Scanlon's or Simon Owens's structures.

## 2. Headlines and email subject lines; what performs

### Takeaway
Substack launched native title A/B testing, but it publishes no aggregate results. Public evidence on subject-line performance is anecdotal, from individual creators. The more careful analysts say to judge titles by clicks per opener, not by open rate alone, because Apple Mail Privacy Protection and hidden opens make open rates unreliable.

### Cited Findings
- **Substack's A/B testing for headlines:** It lets writers test alternate titles on a slice of subscribers and then sends the winner to everyone else. Substack said it tested the feature with a group of bestsellers and was "encouraged by the results and feedback," but gave no numbers. — [On Substack: "New on Substack: A/B testing for headlines…"](https://on.substack.com/p/new-on-substack-ab-testing-for-headlines)
- **Mechanics, per secondary coverage:** Up to 4 title variants. The default test runs 1 hour, and the writer chooses what share of the audience is tested. Reported access at launch was publications with 200+ subscribers. One writer recommends testing only 2–3 variants unless the list is over 10K, because more variants mean smaller samples. — [Online Writing Club](https://www.onlinewritingclub.com/p/substack-now-offers-ab-testing); [Pubstack Success, how-to](https://pubstacksuccess.substack.com/p/how-to-do-title-tests-in-substack?r=nu825)
- **Warning from one experiment:** The plainest title won on open rate, but not by much. A reader warned of possible deliverability issues from title tests "in certain rare circumstances" (snippet). — [Pubstack Success, "My Headline Experiment Results + A Warning About Title Tests"](https://pubstacksuccess.substack.com/p/title-test-my-experiment-results)
- **Creator-level findings (n=1, anecdotal):**
  - In one writer's Substack A/B tests, titles starting with "I" won consistently; one example reached a 23.9% open rate. — [Annabel Gat](https://annabelgat.substack.com/p/substack-ab-testing-how-i-increased)
  - From a CSV export of about 150 emails, one writer found that the top-opening sends all pressed on a tension the reader already felt. — [Mike J Searles](https://mikejsearles.substack.com/p/one-thing-high-open-emails-share-substack)
  - Contrarian subject lines had the lowest open rate but the highest clicks per opener. — reported in search results summarizing [Pubstack Success, "How to Make Your Substack Posts Perform Better"](https://pubstacksuccess.substack.com/p/how-to-make-your-substack-posts-perform)
- **Open-rate reliability:** Substack's own guidance says some inboxes hide opens from senders, which makes open rates inaccurate. A widely shared claim that Apple MPP inflates opens by 10–15% is unsourced. — summarized in search results; see [Pubstack Success stats guide](https://pubstacksuccess.substack.com/p/substack-stats-how-to)
- **Ryan Broderick (Garbage Day):** He has a post literally titled around the "optimal" subject line, which indicates that top writers treat subject lines as a craft topic. Its content was not read. — [Garbage Day](https://garbageday.email/p/this-subject-line-is-the-optimal)
- **Tim Denning:** He has published no headline formula of his own. Secondhand analyses describe headlines that pair a self-deprecating or vulnerable phrase with a concrete money or outcome claim. — [Mehmet Yildiz interview with Denning](https://drmehmetyildiz.substack.com/p/why-did-i-interview-my-aussie-friend); [Illumination analysis](https://medium.com/illumination/i-spent-100-hours-studying-tim-dennings-writing-47b5fd24a2d0)

### Inferences
- Since Substack has published no benchmark, any "subject line = 80% of opens" or "28–50 characters is optimal" claim should be treated as folklore. Those figures appeared only in unsourced skill files and course pages.
- First-person titles ("I…") and tension or contrarian framing are the most-repeated patterns across independent anecdotes. This is weak but convergent evidence.

### Gaps
- I found no large-sample study, academic or from Substack, of Substack headline performance.
- I found no primary statements on subject lines from Lenny, Packy, Newton or Richardson. Richardson's titles are simply dates, such as "October 31, 2026" (background knowledge, unverified here).

## 3. Developing and maintaining a distinctive voice

### Takeaway
The voice advice is consistent across creators. Write constantly, know the subject deeply, be direct or vulnerable, and keep the reader relationship personal. Distinctiveness comes from a stable beat plus a personal lens, not from stylistic tricks.

### Cited Findings
- **Ted Gioia:** "You could only learn to write by writing constantly," and you must learn your subject "because you're going to have to write about something" (snippet). He also says: "It's great to learn the rules. But it's even better to invent your own rules," and "You must control the narrative or it will control you" (snippets). He started The Honest Broker (2021) to honor "that direct connection between writer and reader" (snippet). — [Substack "The Active Voice: Ted Gioia"](https://read.substack.com/p/the-active-voice-ted-gioia); [yespress profile](https://yespress.io/ted-gioia); [Jazz Journal, 2021-04-23](https://jazzjournal.co.uk/2021/04/23/gioia-promises-honest-jazz-coverage/)
- **Gioia's research approach:** Before drafting a survey he starts with big questions: "What changed? What did the music do to lives, society and culture?" (snippet). — [yespress profile](https://yespress.io/ted-gioia) (lower-authority aggregator)
- **Tim Denning:** Analysts attribute his distinctiveness to "not hiding his feelings" and "saying the quiet part out loud." He writes on failure, family, money, mortality and freedom, keeps grammar quirks on purpose, and uses vulnerability as a hook. — [Illumination, 100+ hours studying Denning](https://medium.com/illumination/i-spent-100-hours-studying-tim-dennings-writing-47b5fd24a2d0); [yespress profile](https://yespress.io/tim-denning) (low-authority; quotes unverified)
- **Anne Helen Petersen:** She balances the accessible and the sophisticated, mixing high theory with celebrity and history. — [UCL Arts & Humanities blog](https://blogs.ucl.ac.uk/arts-hums/?p=47); [WMFA podcast](https://wmfapodcast.com/episodes/anne-helen-petersen)
- **Heather Cox Richardson:** Her voice comes from her role as a historian: she places daily news in historical context (implied by the "history's first draft" framing). — [BC Magazine 2022](https://www.bc.edu/content/bc-web/sites/bc-magazine/fall-2022-issue/features/history-s-first-draft.html)
- **Lenny Rachitsky:** He follows "what energizes him" (episode description). — [How I Write, Oct 2023](https://goodpods.com/podcasts/how-i-write-274135/lenny-rachitsky-how-this-ex-airbnb-product-manager-created-a-top-newsl-35478473); for "quality and consistency" as the key to success, see [CNBC, 2024-01-20](https://www.cnbc.com/make-it/2024/01/20/lenny-rachitsky-the-key-to-success-is-quality-and-consistency.html)
- **Packy McCormick:** He enrolled in Write of Passage, David Perell's course, early on. He has said in interviews that he found his voice through flow and a casual, conversational tone. — [The Proof wellness interview](https://theproofwellness.com/packy-mccormick-on-writing-flow-finding-his-voice); [Aakash Gupta](https://www.news.aakashg.com/p/packy-mccormicks-story)

### Inferences
- Voice for these writers is downstream of a clear identity. Richardson writes as a historian, Rachitsky as an ex-Airbnb PM answering questions, Orosz as an ex-Uber engineering manager with insider sources, and Gioia as a critic and musician. Credentialed perspective plus consistency appears to be the durable model.
- For opinion writers such as Yglesias, deBoer and Smith, voice is argumentative and first-person. I could not source their own statements about it here.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve Freddie deBoer's writing advice. A RealClearBooks piece titled "Some Writing Advice" exists, but its content was not visible. — [RealClearBooks, 2023-02-27](https://www.realclearbooks.com/2023/02/27/some_writing_advice_883857.html)
- I found nothing on Kyla Scanlon's voice.

## 4. Drafting and editing process (time, outlines, drafts, editors, checklists)

### Takeaway
Hours per post range from a few hours (daily letters) to more than 100 (flagship deep dives). The writers who sell depth use editors, copy editors, designers, reviewers, and increasingly AI-assisted editing and research. Daily writers such as Richardson and Yglesias rely on volume and routine more than on multiple drafts.

### Cited Findings
- **Lenny Rachitsky: time.** "It takes me a median of 10 hours per post. Sometimes just 3 hours, sometimes 100+ hours" (snippet). A later profile puts it at 5–100 hours per newsletter, "poring over it 50 times before it's published" (snippet). — [Lenny's Newsletter, "500,000"](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/500000); [First Round Review, "Reluctantly Influential"](https://review.firstround.com/reluctantly-influential-inside-lenny-rachitskys-demandingly-chill-life/)
- **Lenny Rachitsky: team.** An editor gives structure and content feedback, plus a copy editor and a designer. His own writing and editing reportedly took 10–30 hours per post (circa 2023). A 2024 CNBC piece says 10–20 hours. — [Growth Curve, Mar 2025](https://www.growthcurvenewsletter.com/p/how-lenny-rachitsky-built-the-world-s-largest-product-newsletter-with-no-full-time-employees); [CNBC, 2024-01-15](https://www.cnbc.com/make-it/2024/01/15/lenny-rachitskys-advice-for-success-in-a-podcast-and-newsletter.html) — **figures conflict by year and source**
- **Lenny Rachitsky: guest posts.** He spends 10+ hours with each guest author editing and tightening, typically over 5–6 iterations. — [Growth Curve, Mar 2025](https://www.growthcurvenewsletter.com/p/how-lenny-rachitsky-built-the-world-s-largest-product-newsletter-with-no-full-time-employees); [1,000,000 post](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/1000000)
- **Lenny Rachitsky: staffing.** As of March 2025 the business had no full-time employees; he uses contractors and volunteers and keeps editorial judgment himself. — [Growth Curve](https://www.growthcurvenewsletter.com/p/how-lenny-rachitsky-built-the-world-s-largest-product-newsletter-with-no-full-time-employees)
- **Lenny Rachitsky: Substack.** On Substack's blog he has been described as a "ruthless editor" with a consistent writing habit. — [On Substack: "How to create a consistent writing habit" (Lenny)](https://on.substack.com/p/how-to-create-consistent-writing-habit-lenny) (not fetched)
- **Gergely Orosz:** Each deep dive takes multiple weeks, covering research, drafting, feedback and editing. He takes notes whenever he gets an idea for a topic. After four years he was hiring an additional tech industry analyst to help with research. — [Lenny's Podcast transcript (via lenny-rag)](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/@mpnikhil/lenny-rag-mcp/blob/b181b8f2cf8a242f98c80f43cae5ce42514d3771/preprocessed/Gergely.json); [Write That Blog](https://writethatblog.substack.com/p/gergely-orosz-on-technical-blogging)
- **Packy McCormick:** His process "has gotten uglier over time." His final check is whether he made a good image. On the "AI & I" podcast (Every) he discussed using AI for thorough research, using Claude to edit his writing, and building interactive visual graphics. In early days, if he had not written enough, he would write "a paragraph or two before falling asleep around 10:30 or 11 PM" (snippet). — [How I Write: "How to publish your best ideas online"](https://metacast.app/podcast/how-i-write/RRY6pAWO/packy-mccormick-how-to-publish-your-best-ideas-online/E1uwO8D4); [AI & I ep. 29 (Every)](https://www.audioscrape.com/podcast/ai-i/episode/how-packy-mccormick-finds-his-next-big-idea-ep-29-37257); [Value Hive ep. 55](https://macro-ops.com/?p=15248); [Danny Miranda podcast](https://dannymiranda.libsyn.com/website/088-packy-mccormick-the-full-time-creator)
- **Heather Cox Richardson:** She writes mostly at night and is often up until 4 or 5 a.m. Producing "1,200 words every day, when you're already working a full-time job is a lot" (snippet). Her mornings start with 1–2 hours of reading social media. — [BC Magazine 2022](https://www.bc.edu/content/bc-web/sites/bc-magazine/fall-2022-issue/features/history-s-first-draft.html); [KALX interview](https://kalx.berkeley.edu/kalx-news-heather-cox-richardson-interview); [The Tyee, 2025-04-04](https://thetyee.ca/Culture/2025/04/04/Hope-America-Heather-Cox-Richardson/)
- **Anne Helen Petersen:** She takes in all the material, takes notes in Scrivener, and then "a route starts to emerge" (snippet; low-authority aggregator). She wrote a dedicated post, "How I Write Culture Study," which is paywalled. — [yespress](https://yespress.io/anne-helen-petersen); [How I Write Culture Study](https://annehelen.substack.com/p/how-i-write-culture-study)
- **Noah Smith:** He says to read first, then write. His PhD advisor's rule was to read many papers for every paper written; for blog posts Smith puts the ratio at "probably around 20 to 1" (snippet). — [Noahpinion, "Noah Smith's writing advice"](https://www.noahpinion.blog/p/noah-smiths-writing-advice)
- **Matt Yglesias:** He has an editor for Slow Boring (Wikipedia names "Crawford"). His process post is paywalled. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Yglesias); [The Slow Boring process](https://www.slowboring.com/p/the-slow-boring-process)
- **Casey Newton:** He walked through his idea-capture-to-publishing workflow on Cortex ep. 171, "State of the Workflow" (Sept 2025). The transcript was not retrieved. — [Pocket Casts listing](https://pocketcasts.com/podcast/cortex/9a61baa0-ec2e-0132-1127-059c869cc4eb/casey-newton-state-of-the-workflow/86e78663-c21c-4a6f-951a-28b9f1355331); [The Enthusiast](https://www.theenthusiast.net/casey-newton-state-of-the-workflow/)
- Related example (not Orosz): Brian Potter of Construction Physics has written a post on his process for researching and writing Substack posts. Search results misattributed it. — noted in search results; URL not confirmed.

### Inferences
- Two process archetypes emerge:
  1. **Daily or volume writers** (Richardson, Yglesias, Smith): a reading-heavy input routine, fast single-pass drafting, minimal external editing, and quality coming from expertise and repetition.
  2. **Depth or flagship writers** (Rachitsky, Orosz, McCormick): many hours to many weeks per post, an outside editor or copy editor, expert reviewers or interviewees, and multiple iterations.
- AI-assisted editing and research (McCormick and Claude) was openly discussed by 2024–2025, which suggests AI is becoming a normal part of the top creators' editing loop.

### Gaps
- I found no published editing checklists from any of these creators.
- Rachitsky's "three rules" from his writing workshop were referenced, but the rules themselves were not retrieved. — [podwise episode summary](https://podwise.ai/dashboard/episodes/7272666)
- I have no specifics on Orosz's reviewer process (for example, whether he sends drafts to the companies he covers).

## 5. Length and format (email vs. web; images, charts, footnotes)

### Takeaway
There is little hard data. Top creators range from about 1,200 words a day (Richardson) to very long deep dives (Not Boring, Pragmatic Engineer). Visuals are treated as a core quality bar by McCormick and Rachitsky, who uses a designer.

### Cited Findings
- **Heather Cox Richardson:** about 1,200 words a day. — [KALX/BC Magazine coverage](https://kalx.berkeley.edu/kalx-news-heather-cox-richardson-interview) (snippet)
- **Not Boring:** It moved to longer, "weedsier" essays. McCormick builds interactive visual graphics, and "did I make a good image" is his final check. — [Aakash Gupta](https://www.news.aakashg.com/p/packy-mccormicks-story); [How I Write episode listing](https://metacast.app/podcast/how-i-write/RRY6pAWO/packy-mccormick-how-to-publish-your-best-ideas-online/E1uwO8D4)
- **Lenny's Newsletter:** A designer is part of the production team. He advises visualizing the idea simply. — [Growth Curve](https://www.growthcurvenewsletter.com/p/how-lenny-rachitsky-built-the-world-s-largest-product-newsletter-with-no-full-time-employees); [podcast summary](https://podcasts-distilled-hugo-content.pages.dev/lennys-podcast-product-career-growth/how-i-built-a-1m-subscriber-newsletter-and-top-10-tech-podcast-lenny-rachitsky/)

### Inferences
- Background knowledge, unverified here: Richardson ends each letter with a list of source links, which works as a footnote substitute, and signs off "Notes:". Very long posts can be clipped by Gmail, so Substack shows a "View entire message" or read-in-app prompt. This matters for very long essays sent by email.

### Gaps
- I found no Substack or third-party data correlating word count with opens, reads or paid conversion.
- I found no data on whether charts or images change engagement on Substack.

## 6. Content characteristics vs. engagement and paid conversion (data)

### Takeaway
Substack's official guidance gives conversion benchmarks: 5–10% of free subscribers convert to paid, with 10% the rate to aim for. It also gives paywall strategies but no post-type breakdown. The only large-sample analysis found is a third-party study of Notes, which shows that what wins free subscribers rarely wins paid ones.

### Cited Findings
- **Substack conversion benchmark:** 5–10% of free subscribers typically convert to paid, with 10% the target. An older Substack checklist says 3–10% of the total free list. — [Substack Going Paid Guide](https://substack.com/going-paid-guide); [On Substack: Going paid checklist](https://on.substack.com/i/63797785/step-announce-paid-subscriptions)
- **What to paywall, per Substack:** There is no fixed formula. Options include keeping the majority of work free (recommended for newer writers) or putting the posts readers engage with most behind the paywall, which can be the "tipping point" for conversion. — [On Substack: "How to convert subscribers from free to paid"](https://on.substack.com/p/free-vs-paid); [On Substack "all free" section](https://on.substack.com/i/77022705/all-free)
- **Third-party Notes study:** Across 396,143 Notes, 0.70% sold at least one paid subscription, versus 13.1% that brought at least one free subscriber. Of the 2,781 most-liked Notes, 7.2% sold a paid subscription. The author concludes that Notes are a weak proxy for paid conversion. (Published about mid-October 2026.) — [The Writing Edge, "Substack Notes That Convert Paid Subscribers: Data Study"](https://thewritingedge.substack.com/p/substack-notes-convert-paid-subscribers)
- **Third-party monetization analysis:** WriteStack says it analyzed about 41,000 writers. Details were not retrieved. — [WriteStack](https://www.writestack.io/blog/how-to-monetize-a-substack)
- **Post-level paywall tactic (opinion, not data):** End the free section at a natural stopping point and put annotated examples or deeper material behind the paywall. — [Substack Course](https://substackcourse.com/how-to-convince-free-substack-readers-to-become-paid-subscribers/)
- **Elle Griffin:** Her viral posts include pieces on the publishing industry, such as "No one buys books," but no conversion numbers were found. — [Sarah Wilson live w/ Elle Griffin](https://sarahwilson.substack.com/p/a-wild-live-with-elle-griffin-on); [The Free Press, "The fight for the future of publishing"](https://www.thefp.com/p/the-fight-for-the-future-of-publishing)
- **Audience-size context (for scale):** Not Boring has 250K+ subscribers ([Value Hive](https://macro-ops.com/?p=15248)). Culture Study counts conflict, at about 105K versus about 184K depending on the date. A low-authority timeline claims Culture Study moved from Substack to Patreon in October 2025; this is unverified. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Helen_Petersen); [Seattle Met, May 2025](https://www.seattlemet.com/news-and-city-life/2025/05/anne-helen-petersen-culture-study); [yespress](https://yespress.io/anne-helen-petersen)

### Inferences
- The available evidence points to depth, utility and exclusive information (scoops, deep dives, templates) as the paid drivers, and to broad, emotional or contrarian short-form content (Notes, viral essays) as the free-growth drivers. This is consistent with Substack's advice to paywall the most-engaged content and with the Notes study, but no study directly ties post characteristics to paid conversion.
