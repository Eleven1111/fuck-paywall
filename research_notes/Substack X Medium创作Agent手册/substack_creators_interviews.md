## Q1. Per creator: core beliefs, rituals, structural heuristics, revision, quality tests, advice to newcomers

### Takeaway
The best-documented craft system is Paul Graham's. His essays describe a full, explicit loop: question with "edge," then a fast bad draft, then strict rereading as a "neutral stranger," then branch-pruning, then the "no briars" publish test. The newsletter writers (Housel, Lenny, Packy, Gioia, Karlsson, Thompson, Cowen) each add one or two signature heuristics on top of that loop. Their heuristics often conflict on two points: whether to write for yourself or for the reader, and whether to polish while drafting or draft fast and fix afterwards.

#### Paul Graham (essays 2005–2024) [PRIMARY-FULLTEXT]
- **Angle = "edge."** "Professional traders won't even trade unless they have what they call an edge… you shouldn't attack a topic unless you have a way in — some new insight about it or way of approaching it. You don't need to have a complete thesis; you just need some kind of gap you can explore. In fact, merely having questions about something other people take for granted can be edge enough." — [The Best Essay, Mar 2024](https://paulgraham.com/best.html)
- **Drafting as converting "vague to bad."** "Writing converts your ideas from vague to bad. But that's a step forward, because once you can see the brokenness, you can fix it." "At least half of essay writing is rereading what you've written and asking *is this correct and complete?*" — [The Best Essay](https://paulgraham.com/best.html)
- **Essay as tree search.** "Usually there are several possible responses to a question, which means you're traversing a tree. But essays are linear… follow whichever offers the greatest combination of generality and novelty." He cut "a 17-paragraph subtree" from that essay. "In general you want to be quick to cut. One of the most dangerous temptations in writing… is to keep something that isn't right, just because it contains a few good bits or cost you a lot of effort." — [The Best Essay](https://paulgraham.com/best.html)
- **Wander first, enforce later.** "Sticking to the question is enforced later, when I notice I've wandered too far and have to backtrack… You don't want the hunt for novelty and generality to be constrained in the moment." — [The Best Essay](https://paulgraham.com/best.html)
- **What makes a good initial question:** "outrageousness… questions that seem naughty in some way — counterintuitive or overambitious or heterodox. Ideally all three." Also: "a good sign if it's one that people think has already been thoroughly explored," and "you have to be interested in it." — [The Best Essay](https://paulgraham.com/best.html)
- **Ideas can't be generated from a topic list.** "To start writing an essay, you need a topic plus some initial insight about it, and you can't generate those systematically." Instead, improve inputs in "breadth" (reading, talking to "the sort of people who make you have new ideas," travel) and "depth" (doing hard work in some other domain): "to be a good essayist you also have to do, or have done, some other kind of work." — [The Best Essay](https://paulgraham.com/best.html)
- **When it's done.** "At some point the cycle of question and response reaches what feels like a natural end… you start to feel sated… the reader is probably feeling sated too." — [The Best Essay](https://paulgraham.com/best.html). In 2005: "learn to recognize the approach of an ending, and when one appears, grab it." — [Writing, Briefly](https://paulgraham.com/writing44.html)
- **Evergreen paradox.** To stay surprising for future readers, write "about things people never learn," meaning mistakes people "never learn to avoid except by making them." — [The Best Essay](https://paulgraham.com/best.html)
- **The "neutral stranger" test.** "You have to pretend to be a neutral reader who knows nothing of what's in your head… does it seem correct? Does it seem complete?… It takes me many cycles before I can get an essay past the stranger. But the stranger is rational, so you always can, if you ask him what he needs." He spends "2 weeks on an essay and reread[s] drafts 50 times." "Half the ideas that end up in an essay will be ones you thought of while you were writing it." — [Putting Ideas into Words, Feb 2022](https://paulgraham.com/words.html)
- **The usefulness formula.** "Useful writing tells people something true and important that they didn't already know, and tells them as unequivocally as possible… the four components are like numbers you can multiply together." "Useful writing makes claims that are as strong as they can be made without becoming false." — [How to Write Usefully, Feb 2020](https://paulgraham.com/useful.html)
- **The Morris technique (publication bias as a virtue).** "If you write a bad sentence, you don't publish it. You delete it and try again. Often you abandon whole branches of four or five paragraphs. Sometimes a whole essay." — [How to Write Usefully](https://paulgraham.com/useful.html)
- **The "briars" readiness test.** "My strategy is loose, then tight… there are sentences I've read 100 times before publishing them… passages that stick out in an annoying way… They become like briars that catch your sleeve… I won't publish an essay till they're all gone… I will never knowingly let through one that doesn't seem correct." — [How to Write Usefully](https://paulgraham.com/useful.html)
- **Handling misreadings.** Forestall misinterpretations "a reasonably smart and well-intentioned person might make." Do not forestall intentional misreadings in the body ("An essay is a place to meet honest readers. You don't want to… put bars on the windows"); put those defenses in end-notes. "Brevity is the diction of command." — [How to Write Usefully](https://paulgraham.com/useful.html)
- **Beginner advice.** Relax the "importance" constraint by narrowing the topic: "If you only have ten readers who care, that's fine." He wrote unpublished notebook essays "for about 15 years." — [How to Write Usefully](https://paulgraham.com/useful.html)
- **The 2005 tactical checklist.** The essay took 67 minutes: 23 writing, 44 rewriting. Points include:
  - "Write a bad version 1 as fast as you can"
  - "if you can't get started, tell someone what you plan to write about, then write down what you said"
  - "expect 80% of the ideas in an essay to happen after you start writing it, and 50% of those you start with to be wrong"
  - "start writing when you think of the first sentence; if a deadline forces you to start before that, just say the most important sentence first"
  - "read your essays out loud to see (a) where you stumble… and (b) which bits are boring (the paragraphs you dread reading)"
  - "when you finish, leave yourself something easy to start with"
  - "write for a reader who won't read the essay as carefully as you do, just as pop songs are designed to sound ok on crappy car radios"
  - "ask friends which sentence you'll regret most"
  - "learn to distinguish surprises from digressions"
  - "use footnotes to contain digressions"
  
  — [Writing, Briefly, Mar 2005](https://paulgraham.com/writing44.html)
- **Simplicity as honesty.** "Fancy writing doesn't just conceal ideas. It can also conceal the lack of them… If you say nothing simply, it will be obvious to everyone, including you." On complex sentences or fancy words: "you should never do it by accident." — [Write Simply, Mar 2021](https://paulgraham.com/simply.html)
- **The AI-era claim.** "In a couple decades there won't be many people who can write… writing is thinking… a world of thinks and think-nots." It quotes Lamport: "If you're thinking without writing, you only think you're thinking." — [Writes and Write-Nots, Oct 2024](https://paulgraham.com/writes.html)
- **On How I Write (reported quotes):** "Pay attention to what you find interesting. Not what other people think is gonna be interesting." "When you find yourself very curious about an apparently minor question, that's an exciting sign." "What seems like work to other people that doesn't seem like work to you?" "You should expect to throw away a lot of your early work." "Don't try to develop a personal style… Just try to write well. The style will emerge." — [SECONDARY-GITHUB](https://github.com/AlonMichael/How-I-Write-Skills/blob/main/catalog.md)

#### Morgan Housel (Collab Fund; How I Write; Tim Ferriss #702, Oct/Nov 2023)
- **"Selfish writing" / audience of one.** He writes only what he personally finds interesting. He names "know your reader" as the worst writing advice, because "knowing your audience" slides into pandering, "the worst kind of writing." — [Perell podcast page](https://perell.com/podcast/morgan-housel-writing-for-the-internet/) [SNIPPET]; [Ferriss #702 transcript](https://tim.blog/2023/11/02/morgan-housel-transcript/) [SNIPPET]; [Writer Science, "The Single Worst Piece of Writing Advice"](https://www.writerscience.com/the-single-worst-piece-of-writing-advice/) [SNIPPET]
- **"First draft and publish"** is his self-described "probably the worst writing advice." He edits each sentence as he types, asking whether a better word exists, rather than drafting fast and revising later. For him, writing doubles as the research process. — [Ferriss #702](https://tim.blog/2023/11/02/morgan-housel-transcript/) [SNIPPET]; [Bullet Point summary of How I Write](https://bulletpointpodcast.com/howiwrite/how-to-write-ridiculously-well-morgan-housel-interview/) [SNIPPET]
- **"Pretend every word costs you $100."** "People don't remember books, they remember sentences." He quotes Elmore Leonard: "leave out the parts that readers tend to skip." He calls "In summary" the worst possible last sentence. — [SECONDARY-GITHUB](https://github.com/AlonMichael/How-I-Write-Skills/blob/main/catalog.md); [Bullet Point summary](https://bulletpointpodcast.com/howiwrite/how-to-write-ridiculously-well-morgan-housel-interview/) [SNIPPET]
- **Natural unit of thought.** His first Psychology of Money chapters ran 6,000 words and failed ("I wrote two shitty chapters… friends… wrote back, 'What is this? This is not you.'"). His fix: "I'm a blogger… each chapter is a long blog post. I just owned that." Also: "Even within a 10,000-word piece, there are ten mini blogs within there." — [SECONDARY-GITHUB, structure/format skills](https://github.com/AlonMichael/How-I-Write-Skills)
- **Angle via storytelling.** "If you are just writing as a data person, you're never gonna stick out. But if you could tell a story about finance, you might actually stick out." "Everywhere you look, it's best story wins." — [SECONDARY-GITHUB](https://github.com/AlonMichael/How-I-Write-Skills)
- **Volume apprenticeship.** About 3,500 articles over roughly 9–10 years at the Motley Fool, about three a day. "If you publish every day and you get feedback every day… you are forced to see what works and what doesn't." On his early writing: "It was not enjoyable at first. It felt just like a job." — [SECONDARY-GITHUB](https://github.com/AlonMichael/How-I-Write-Skills); [Bullet Point summary](https://bulletpointpodcast.com/howiwrite/how-to-write-ridiculously-well-morgan-housel-interview/) [SNIPPET]
- **Satisfaction ratio.** "I derive 90% of the satisfaction from how hard I worked on it, and 10% from how it did." — [SECONDARY-GITHUB distribute skill](https://github.com/AlonMichael/How-I-Write-Skills/blob/main/skills/distribute/SKILL.md)
- **Primary essay to fetch later:** "The Written Word" (Collab Fund). — [collabfund.com/blog/the-written-word](https://collabfund.com/blog/the-written-word/) (not read; blocked)

#### Packy McCormick (Not Boring; How I Write, ~Feb 2024)
- **"I write the piece, and then I write the piece."** A V0 draft gets scrapped and becomes a dump for cut material. He writes six or seven drafts, often starting a fresh doc each time. Recently he has rewritten whole pieces from 4:30 to 8:55 AM on publication morning. He "needs intense time pressure" and moved his send from Monday to Tuesday to get more runway. — [SECONDARY-GITHUB format-adapt skill](https://github.com/AlonMichael/How-I-Write-Skills/blob/main/skills/format-adapt/SKILL.md); [Bullet Point summary](https://bulletpointpodcast.com/howiwrite/packy-mc-cormick-a-tactical-masterclass-in-online-writing/) [SNIPPET]
- **The emotional arc of each essay.** A week-long cycle: excitement, research, the realization that the topic is bigger than expected, renewed excitement, then writing. Topic chosen around Wed/Thu; weekend "in the hole" researching and writing. — [Bullet Point](https://bulletpointpodcast.com/howiwrite/packy-mc-cormick-a-tactical-masterclass-in-online-writing/) [SNIPPET]; [Writing with Ayo spotlight](https://writingwithayo.substack.com/p/spotlighting-packy-mccormick) [SNIPPET]
- **First-reader test.** His wife reads drafts to judge whether they're interesting. His wife and brother were his early editors. — same Bullet Point summary; [Perell, Ultimate Guide to Writing Online](https://perell.com/essay/the-ultimate-guide-to-writing-online/) [SNIPPET]
- **Distribution craft.** "A really good bar chart or shocking stat" outperforms thumbnails when promoting on X. — [SECONDARY-GITHUB distribute skill](https://github.com/AlonMichael/How-I-Write-Skills/blob/main/skills/distribute/SKILL.md)
- He credits Write of Passage: "I never would have started writing online if I hadn't met @david_perell and taken Write of Passage." — [Packy X](https://x.com/packyM/status/1762857572095140027)

#### Ted Gioia (The Honest Broker; How I Write ~May 2024; own essays)
- **Golden rule: "Always be honest."** Three critique questions: Is it fair? Is it accurate? Would it persuade all fair-minded observers? — [Perell thread](https://x.com/david_perell/status/1795800774254985236) [SNIPPET]
- **Writer's-block cure.** A private honest journal: buy a blank journal and hide it; each day write what happened in the last 24 hours with no sugarcoating, for a month. Gioia presents it as advice he once received. — [Perell X](https://x.com/david_perell/status/1795893535717081570); [Richard Rabil, Mar 2024](https://richardrabil.com/2024/03/15/ted-gioias-advice-about-honest-writing/) [SNIPPET]
- **"I Give the Worst Writing Advice in the World"** (c. late 2024, started as a private email reply). If he had chased audience size he would have quit decades ago. Seek motivation in the writing itself, then "find a reader, make a connection," since even one reader is meaningful. — [Honest Broker](https://www.honest-broker.com/p/i-give-the-worst-writing-advice-in) [SNIPPET]
- **Become an expert first.** Find "one or two areas in which they know more than other writers." Persuasion "only works if people trust you." — [Athens summary](https://tryathens.com/blog/ted-gioia-writing-advice) [SNIPPET, secondhand]; [Substack "Active Voice"](https://post.substack.com/p/the-active-voice-ted-gioia) [SNIPPET]; [Writing Routines interview](https://www.writingroutines.com/ted-gioia-interview/) [SNIPPET]

#### Henrik Karlsson (Escaping Flatland; How I Write)
- **Gather "raw footage" before drafting.** "If he thinks he's writing the essay, he gets tense and starts polishing the prose too early." — [On the preparations before writing an essay](https://www.henrikkarlsson.xyz/p/on-the-preparations-before-writing) [SNIPPET]
- **A method helps, but the best material comes from breaking it.** — [How I write essays](https://www.henrikkarlsson.xyz/p/how-i-write-essays) [SNIPPET]
- **Let ideas "live in my body for about a year."** Critique that comes too early "squishes the ideas." He has a five-step method that starts with pressure-free exploration and crafts the opening last (steps not retrieved). — [How I Write: "The Writing Process Nobody Teaches You"](https://howiwrite.substack.com/p/henrik-karlsson-the-writing-process) [SNIPPET]
- **Quality versus effort.** Spending twice as long on a post yields on average more than four times the new subscribers (Karlsson's own data as reported by Newport). — [Cal Newport, On Slow Writing](https://calnewport.com/on-slow-writing/) [SNIPPET]
- **Voice.** "It is the stuff about you that is odd that is interesting." You develop a feel for what you do "after about 50 essays." — [Substack note](https://substack.com/@henrikkarlsson/note/c-87287315) [SNIPPET]
- **Sequence of a good essay.** Rich experience or deep knowledge, then an insight, then supporting stories surface, then unity as he moves the pieces around. — [Substack note](https://substack.com/@henrikkarlsson/note/c-16682185) [SNIPPET]

### Inferences
- PG's loop is the most transferable "source code," for three reasons. It is explicit (question with edge, fast draft, strict reread, prune, briars test). It is repeated consistently across 2005–2024. And it can be read verbatim.
- The newsletter-native writers add three constraints PG doesn't face:
  1. a cadence or deadline (Thompson daily, Lenny and Packy weekly)
  2. the free versus paid reader split (Thompson)
  3. a promotion asset per piece (Packy's chart or stat)
- Housel and Packy show that "readiness" for deadline writers is partly defined by the clock. For PG and Karlsson, who have no deadline, it is defined by the absence of friction ("briars," "sated").

## Q2. Recurring cross-creator principles and disagreements

### Takeaway
There is strong consensus on five things: writing is thinking, ideas arrive mid-draft, cutting is the core skill, volume builds skill, and you should write what genuinely interests you. The sharpest disagreements are about audience (Housel's "selfish writing" versus PG's reader-as-stranger, Thompson's free/paid calibration and Lenny's usefulness), drafting mode (polish while drafting versus fast bad v1), and incubation time (Karlsson's year versus daily or weekly deadline writers).

### Cited Findings
**Consensus**
- **Writing generates ideas; it doesn't just transcribe them.**
  - PG: "expect 80% of the ideas… after you start writing" ([Writing, Briefly](https://paulgraham.com/writing44.html)); "Half the ideas… thought of while you were writing" ([Putting Ideas into Words](https://paulgraham.com/words.html)).
  - Housel: the writing process doubles as research ([Ferriss #702](https://tim.blog/2023/11/02/morgan-housel-transcript/) [SNIPPET]).
  - Kevin Kelly: "Write to find what you think" ([catalog](https://github.com/AlonMichael/How-I-Write-Skills/blob/main/catalog.md)).
- **Interest or energy as the topic filter.** PG ("you have to care," [Best Essay](https://paulgraham.com/best.html)); Housel ("selfish writing"); Lenny ("follow what energizes him," [Perell](https://x.com/david_perell/status/1717138268444090672)); Gioia (intrinsic motivation, [Honest Broker](https://www.honest-broker.com/p/i-give-the-worst-writing-advice-in)).
- **Ruthless cutting.** PG ("quick to cut," [Best Essay](https://paulgraham.com/best.html)); Housel ("$100 per word"); Lenny (cut every unnecessary word; drop the first paragraph); Ferriss (the 10% to cut).
- **Volume is the apprenticeship.** Housel's 3,500 articles; PG's 15 years of notebooks ([Useful](https://paulgraham.com/useful.html)); Karlsson's "about 50 essays" ([note](https://substack.com/@henrikkarlsson/note/c-87287315)); Cowen's daily blogging for 18+ years.
- **Read aloud or use a first reader.** PG reads aloud to find "the paragraphs you dread reading" ([Writing, Briefly](https://paulgraham.com/writing44.html)); Orlean reads aloud; Packy's wife acts as interest-tester.
- **Depth outside writing gives you the edge.** PG: "you also have to do, or have done, some other kind of work" ([Best Essay](https://paulgraham.com/best.html)); Gioia: become an expert first ([Athens](https://tryathens.com/blog/ted-gioia-writing-advice) [SNIPPET]).

**Disagreements**
- **Audience.** Housel calls "know your reader" the worst advice and writes for an audience of one ([Writer Science](https://www.writerscience.com/the-single-worst-piece-of-writing-advice/) [SNIPPET]). PG instead requires passing a neutral stranger and writing for the "crappy car radio" reader ([Putting Ideas into Words](https://paulgraham.com/words.html), [Writing, Briefly](https://paulgraham.com/writing44.html)). Thompson calibrates assumed knowledge differently for free and paid readers. *Reconciliation:* Housel's own remarks argue against the audience steering topic choice, not against clarity (per the search summary of the [Perell page](https://perell.com/podcast/morgan-housel-writing-for-the-internet/) [SNIPPET]).
- **Drafting mode.** PG: "loose, then tight," with a fast bad v1 ([Useful](https://paulgraham.com/useful.html)); Pressfield: "cover the canvas." Housel instead edits each sentence as he goes and calls his approach "first draft and publish" ([Ferriss #702](https://tim.blog/2023/11/02/morgan-housel-transcript/) [SNIPPET]). Karlsson is in between: gather raw footage, and avoid polishing prose too early.
- **Draft count.** Packy writes 6–7 drafts, starting fresh docs each time; Lenny takes 10–30 passes; PG rereads 50–100 times. Housel, by contrast, does near-single-pass writing.
- **Incubation.** Karlsson lets ideas live in his body for about a year ([How I Write](https://howiwrite.substack.com/p/henrik-karlsson-the-writing-process) [SNIPPET]). PG mulls "for a few days." Thompson works to a daily deadline; Packy writes from a weekly topic choice with a rewrite on publication morning.
- **Planning.** PG: "don't (always) make detailed outlines." Lee Child never outlines ("I've told myself the story and I'm bored with it"). Orlean uses 700 index cards. Lenny uses a Coda dump page per topic.
- **Time pressure.** Packy needs intense deadline pressure. PG says essayists' advantage over journalists is "You don't have a deadline" ([Useful](https://paulgraham.com/useful.html)).

### Inferences
- The audience disagreement mostly dissolves if you split it into stages. Topic selection is "selfish" (interest-driven). Execution is reader-strict (stranger test, cut what readers skip).
- Drafting-mode differences track a writer's natural unit and cadence. Short-unit writers (Housel's 1,500-word blogs) can polish inline. Writers producing long synthesized deep dives (Packy, Lenny) need multiple full rewrites.

### Gaps
- None of these sources contained quantitative evidence on which drafting mode produces better results, apart from Karlsson's self-reported "2x time leads to >4x subscribers."

## Q3. Which principles can become agent checklists or prompts, and which must stay human

### Takeaway
PG's tests are especially automatable as critic prompts: the neutral-stranger read, correct/complete checks, strength-without-falsehood, true × important × novel × strong scoring, briars detection, surprise-versus-digression, and footnoting digressions. So are the line-level rules: $100/word, cut the first paragraph, no "In summary," simple Germanic words. What must stay human is the edge (the initial insight), genuine interest or energy, lived depth from other work, honesty judgments about one's own experience, and the "sated" sense of an ending.

### Cited Findings (source principle, then agent form)
**Automatable (critic or checklist prompts)**
- **Neutral-stranger review:** "does it seem correct? Does it seem complete?… ask him what he needs" ([Putting Ideas into Words](https://paulgraham.com/words.html)). Agent form: a fresh-context reviewer with no access to notes lists each missing premise, unqualified claim and confusing jump.
- **Usefulness score:** true × important × novel × strong, with claims "as strong as they can be made without becoming false" ([Useful](https://paulgraham.com/useful.html)). Agent form: score each claim, flagging both vague hedges ("the issue is complex") and overclaims.
- **Briars pass:** repeated reads to flag passages that "catch your sleeve" because they are clumsy or possibly untrue; never publish a sentence that doesn't seem correct ([Useful](https://paulgraham.com/useful.html)). Agent form: multiple sampled read-throughs, then a highlight-friction report.
- **Branch pruning:** follow branches with the highest "generality and novelty," be "quick to cut," and separate "surprises from digressions," moving digressions to footnotes ([Best Essay](https://paulgraham.com/best.html), [Writing, Briefly](https://paulgraham.com/writing44.html)). Agent form: outline-tree view; flag subtrees that stray from the initial question.
- **Line rules:**
  - $100 per word; "leave out the parts readers tend to skip"; no "In summary" endings (Housel, [catalog](https://github.com/AlonMichael/How-I-Write-Skills/blob/main/catalog.md))
  - delete the first paragraph of draft 1 (Lenny, [SNIPPET](https://manual.compoundplanning.com/chapters/interview-with-lenny-rachitsky-writer-of-lennys-newsletter))
  - simple words, no fancy diction "by accident" ([Write Simply](https://paulgraham.com/simply.html))
  - "say the most important sentence first" under deadline ([Writing, Briefly](https://paulgraham.com/writing44.html))
- **Misreading guard:** pre-empt misreadings by "a reasonably smart and well-intentioned person"; put defenses against bad-faith readings in footnotes, not the body ([Useful](https://paulgraham.com/useful.html)). Agent form: a steelman-misreader persona.
- **Gioia's fairness triad:** fair? accurate? persuasive to all fair-minded observers? ([Perell thread](https://x.com/david_perell/status/1795800774254985236) [SNIPPET]). Agent form: a three-question rubric.
- **Format-tier calibration:** a free piece needs a hook and assumes less knowledge; paid pieces assume more (Thompson). Also "second article" linking (Thompson); one chart or stat asset per piece for promotion (Packy).
- **Process scaffolding:**
  - an end-of-session "what's next" note (Lenny/Perell, [X](https://x.com/lennysan/status/1347642510445711360))
  - a per-topic idea dump page (Lenny, [X](https://x.com/lennysan/status/1347642545321308160))
  - a V0 cut-material file (Packy)
  - "tell someone what you plan to write, then write down what you said," which an agent can run as an interview-then-transcribe mode ([Writing, Briefly](https://paulgraham.com/writing44.html))

**Must stay human (or human-in-the-loop)**
- **The edge or initial insight:** "you can't generate those systematically. If only." ([Best Essay](https://paulgraham.com/best.html)). An agent can surface candidate "gaps" (assumptions others take for granted) but cannot supply the writer's insight.
- **Genuine interest:** "to stretch for the novel insights… you have to care" ([Best Essay](https://paulgraham.com/best.html)); Housel's selfish filter; Lenny's energy test.
- **Depth from doing other hard work** (PG); expertise "in one or two areas" (Gioia).
- **Voice and idiosyncrasy:** Cowen keeps writing his own and warns against AI smoothing; Karlsson says "the stuff about you that is odd"; PG says style emerges from trying to write well.
- **Honesty about lived experience** (Gioia's private journal) and incubation time (Karlsson's year).
- **The "sated" ending feeling and the ending-grab** (PG). An agent can propose candidate endings; the final call rests on the writer's sense of completion.
- **The thinking itself:** PG's "thinks and think-nots" warning ([Writes and Write-Nots](https://paulgraham.com/writes.html)) and Ezra Klein's "case against writing with AI" episode ([catalog](https://github.com/AlonMichael/How-I-Write-Skills/blob/main/catalog.md)) both imply that agent drafting should not replace the human's question-and-response loop.

### Inferences
- A good agent design mirrors PG's "loose, then tight":
  1. The human, or a human-led interview, produces a fast v1 with real edge.
  2. Agents run the tight phase: stranger review, briars pass, usefulness scoring, line rules, misreading guard, format calibration.
  3. The human decides on cuts and branches and owns the ending.
- An existing open-source attempt (AlonMichael/How-I-Write-Skills: 10 skills covering ideate, research, structure, hook, draft, storytelling, edit-structure, edit-line, format-adapt and distribute, plus SOUL.md and good/bad example outputs) can serve as a reference or competitor for the handbook. Its quotes still need verification against the original audio.

### Gaps
- I found no creator here who publicly describes using an agent checklist of this kind. Packy uses Claude for editing (per existing notes), but no specific prompt or checklist was retrievable.
- I could not verify how Perell's own Write of Passage curriculum operationalizes these ideas; perell.com and writeofpassage.com were blocked.
