## Q1. Per creator: core beliefs, routines and volume, named frameworks, editing and quality tests, advice (with quotes and dates)

### Takeaway
The craft beliefs that recur and are well sourced:
- **Angle and voice matter more than new ideas.** Denning says he "attack[s] ideas from unusual viewpoints." Perell calls himself a "seed stage investor in ideas."
- **Pick one specific reader.** Perell's one-sheet, Günel's "Jennifer" persona and Medium's Creators Hub all say this.
- **Draft badly and fast, then edit separately.** See Abdaal's FBR, Eliason's "shitty draft," Foroux's no-edit timer and Denning's separate editing days.
- **Set a low daily bar for consistency.** Foroux, Göke and Perell all say this.

On AI, every writer covered here moved (2024–2026) to some version of "emotion, lived stories and rule-breaking are what's left." Perell went furthest: he shut down Write of Passage (announced Aug 2024) and said in Feb 2025 he stopped teaching "partially because of the AI wave."

### Cited Findings

#### Tim Denning (Medium, then Substack; about 10 years and 5,000+ essays by his own count)
- **Angle over novelty [V, 2024-04-29]:** "The skill I figured out by accident isn't to have new ideas. No. It's to attack ideas from unusual viewpoints. It's your view of the world and the angles you take on stories that make you successful." — [timdenning.com: Why Tim Denning Writes like Sh*t and Is Rich](https://timdenning.com/tim-denning-is-rich/) (via [GitHub corpus](https://github.com/jpeslar1/john-peslar-ai-skills))
- **People over niches [V, 2024]:** "Readers buy humans, not niches." — [same post](https://timdenning.com/tim-denning-is-rich/)
  - A Substack Note [S], undated in the snippet, says the same: "20 writing lessons I learned after 10 years of writing and 5000+ essays: You don't need a f*cking niche. Nobody gives a damn about your writing. All they care about is whether you can help, inspire, educate, or entertain them. If the headline isn't easy to understand…" — [Denning Substack Note](https://substack.com/@timdenning/note/c-64723966)
- **AI and voice [V, 2024]:** "In a world of AI, you can't outwrite ChatGPT. So all you have left is your writer's voice and your bank of stories trapped inside your sexy head that must escape before you're dead." — [timdenning.com](https://timdenning.com/tim-denning-is-rich/)
- **Headline tests [V, 2024-03-25]:** "Make your headline so easy to understand that a 5th grader gets it. Ask yourself: 'Why should someone click this?'" He also rejects hashtags outright ("For the love of god… No more hashtags"). — [timdenning.com: 16 Harsh Truths About Your Writing](https://timdenning.com/writing-harsh-truths/)
- **Distribution reality [V, 2024]:** "There's published content, and there's content people can actually see on the platform. The two are not the same." — [16 Harsh Truths](https://timdenning.com/writing-harsh-truths/)
- **Routine [S, his own 2022 post per snippet]:**
  - He walks so headlines and ideas can develop, then brain-dumps them.
  - He writes the **headline and bullet points before drafting**.
  - He **writes and edits on different days**.
  - He recommends writing **5 headlines a day** to build the skill.
  - Sources: [timdenning.com: writing system](https://timdenning.com/the-key-to-become-a-wildly-successful-writer-with-millions-of-views-is-a-writing-system/); [How I Write 480,000 Words a Month and Never Run out of Ideas](https://timdenning.com/never-run-out-of-ideas/)
  - The "480,000 words a month" figure comes from the post's title only. It is about 16k words a day, which is implausible as pure drafting, so it probably counts more than drafting. Not verified.
- **Batch schedule (third-party, conflicting) [S]:** One analyst says he writes on Tuesdays and Thursdays, five articles each day. Another says Wednesdays and Saturdays, 8am–6pm, five articles a day. — [abitgamey: Six ways Tim Denning wrote his way to freedom](https://abitgamey.substack.com/p/six-ways-tim-denning-wrote-his-way); [Illumination: I tried Tim Denning's routine](https://medium.com/illumination/i-tried-tim-dennings-writing-routine-for-a-week-and-failed-lessons-i-ve-learnt-61f37c2cf8d); [neeramahajan: deconstructed 100 articles](https://neeramahajan.com/i-deconstructed-100-of-tim-dennings-articles-and-this-is-what-i-learned/)
- **Spoken, podcast 2023-05-22 [V-ASR]:**
  - "I think that people follow you based on your view of the world… the second thing which annoys people is I tell people that everything is connected to everything."
  - On cross-posting: "copy and pasting doesn't work because every one of these platforms… it's like learning a language."
  - On beginners: "you're writing emails every day… All I'm asking you to do is take what you're already doing and just post it on the internet."
  - On products: "Everything is data backed."
  - Source: [Small Business Big Marketing #637](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/how-writing-changed-tim-dennings-business-life-forever-637/id319019197?i=1000613999291)
- **2026 positioning: "unhinged" vs. polished [V, 2026-10-16]:** "Polished is forgettable. Unhinged is never forgotten." He also says: "This isn't a how-to post. It's a call to arms." — [Substack: Not being unhinged is quietly costing you everything](https://timdenning.substack.com/p/not-being-unhinged-is-quietly-costing)
- **AI craft stance, 2025–26 [S]:**
  - "AI can't do emotions," so adding emotion is an easy way to avoid sounding like AI slop. Not everything needs emotion, but some of it should.
  - He rejects hacks and copywriting formulas and mocks cookie-cutter conclusions.
  - He recommends Tim Ferriss's trick of drafting as an email to a friend in Gmail.
  - One section is titled "Write where good writing exists," meaning environment shapes quality.
  - Source: [Substack: How to Write Well (In the Age of AI Slop)](https://timdenning.substack.com/p/how-to-write-well-in-the-age-of-ai)
- **More AI and career views [S]:**
  - "If AI does the thinking for you, then you're not thinking."
  - AI is "an enhancer of good humans and a replacement for lazy humans."
  - Extraordinary writers write paragraph-long prompts.
  - A 2026 career piece says AI "can now write better than 90% of writers," so "I write best" won't make millions. Use writing to help people and build an audience.
  - Sources: [Adults Have Totally Misunderstood How AI Will Affect Society](https://timdenning.substack.com/p/adults-have-totally-misunderstood); [How to build a writing career that makes $1M+ a year](https://timdenning.substack.com/p/how-to-build-a-writing-career-that); [The "Writer's Dream" Is Dead](https://timdenning.substack.com/p/the-writers-dream-is-dead)
- **Observed style signature [V-derived; corpus compiler's estimates, not Denning's claims]:**
  - Median sentence is about 9 words, and about 60% of paragraphs are one line.
  - Hooks: confession about 30%, contrarian verdict about 25%, credential-by-numbers about 15%.
  - Profanity is always asterisked. No emoji and no hashtags.
  - Source: [GitHub corpus derived stats](https://github.com/jpeslar1/john-peslar-ai-skills)

#### Zulie Rane (writer and YouTuber; later Medium staff as "Zulie @ Medium")
- **Writer's block [S]:** after more than two years of trying almost every strategy, only **two** work for her. One is for *starting* and one for *continuing*, and each takes about 5 minutes a day. — [Zulie Writes: 2 writer's block strategies I use](https://medium.com/zulie-writes/2-writers-block-strategies-i-use-df53b6771c33). The two techniques themselves were not visible in the snippet.
- **Routine [S, older interview]:** she writes when she feels like it, works best in the late afternoon in a quiet space near home, and gets ideas from her cats, TikTok trends and medical journals. — [Writing Cooperative: Write Now with Zulie Rane](https://medium.com/writing-cooperative/write-now-with-zulie-rane-b4746e33bdc0)
- **Burnout guard, the 20% rule [S]:**
  - She spends about 20% of her time on unprofitable writing.
  - Weekly, she drives to a town where nobody knows her, turns off her phone and works only on a novel.
  - She argues that hustle alone makes writing miserable.
  - Source: [Zulie Writes: Why I Spend 20% Of My Time on Unprofitable Writing Work](https://www.zuliewrites.com/blog/why-i-spend-20-of-my-writing-time-on-unprofitable-work)
- **Curation, as Medium staff [S]:**
  - Curators are "browsing 7 days a week."
  - "Using topics can help surface it to our curators," and writers should read the distribution guidelines thoroughly.
  - Separately: "curators don't actually see tags," per Medium Support.
  - Sources: [Zulie @ Medium reply](https://medium.com/@zulie_at_medium/great-question-a73c507e111f); [Zulie Writes: Six Common Misconceptions About Medium Curation](https://www.zuliewrites.com/blog/six-common-misconceptions-about-medium-curation)

#### Shannon Ashley (personal essay, memoir; Medium)
- **Honesty over polish [S]:** "it's not about being flawless, it's about being honest." She also says readers value authenticity. — [Shannon Ashley: My Rules for Authentic and Vulnerable Writing](https://medium.com/@Shesreallyfat/my-rules-for-authentic-and-vulnerable-writing-364c0aeeb5ce); [Writing Cooperative: Advice From 15 Successful Medium Writers](https://writingcooperative.com/advice-from-15-successful-medium-writers-thatll-make-you-a-better-writer-fbedbd7767c1)
- **Vulnerability is the writer's choice [S]:** the decision to "tell all, tell most, tell anything" is always the writer's own. — [same Rules post](https://medium.com/@Shesreallyfat/my-rules-for-authentic-and-vulnerable-writing-364c0aeeb5ce)
- **Emotional cost and pacing [S]:** vulnerable stories leave you "emotionally spent," so you need recovery time. She can't write emotionally vulnerable pieces every day. — [same](https://medium.com/@Shesreallyfat/my-rules-for-authentic-and-vulnerable-writing-364c0aeeb5ce)
- **Process [S]:** she drafts on her phone or tablet, rewrites as she goes, and publishes quickly before she can "chicken out or second-guess" herself. — [same](https://medium.com/@Shesreallyfat/my-rules-for-authentic-and-vulnerable-writing-364c0aeeb5ce)
- **Burnout, 2025 [S]:**
  - A 2025 post, "I Rebranded My Life's Work," says she needed something "softer, wilder—and more true" after years of writing through trauma.
  - Another post says the online writing landscape "crushed my joy for writing anything at all."
  - A third piece is titled "I'm a Tired Writer with Autism Burnout."
  - Sources: [Ashley on Writing: Maybe I'm Not Really a Writer](https://medium.com/ashley-on-writing/maybe-im-not-really-a-writer-5d72b658fe69); [Honestly Yours: I'm a Tired Writer with Autism Burnout](https://medium.com/honestly-yours/im-a-tired-writer-with-autism-burnout-fdf4f4f54707)

- **Structure first [S, third-party write-up of his system]:**

  - Steps: brainstorm the topic, its subpoints and the headline, then free-write without editing.
  - Source: [The Kickstarter: How to Write Like A #1 Medium.com Writer — Ben Hardy's System](https://medium.com/the-kickstarter/how-to-write-like-a-1-medium-com-writer-ben-hardys-system-916dd720069b)

  - He writes only when in the right mental and emotional state.
  - His mornings start with about 20 minutes of journaling.
  - In 2017 he wrote that sketching article ideas in his journal makes the actual article "much faster and easier."
  - Sources: [My Morning Routine: Benjamin Hardy](https://mymorningroutine.com/benjamin-hardy/); [Inc. (2017)](https://www.inc.com/benjamin-p-hardy/how-i-use-my-journal-to-create-my-future-achieve-my-goals.html)
- **Format and persistence [S]:**
  - List articles "still do well" and are a good way to organize a piece.
  - It took about 15–20 articles before one took off.
  - He cross-posted from his own blog to Medium.
  - Sources: [Smart Passive Income ep. 431](https://www.smartpassiveincome.com/podcasts/writing-still-viable-content-platform-medium-copywriting-tips-benjamin-hardy/); a podcast summary claims his email list grew from 0 to 400k via Medium: [EO 360°](https://entrepreneursorg.libsyn.com/ben-hardy-number-1-writer-on-medium)

No transcript was found. — [LWS #005](https://londonwriterssalon.simplecast.com/episodes/005-ben-hardy-mastering-articles-future-self-dRKVtnmd)

#### Darius Foroux (writing online since 2015; 600+ articles, 8 books, more than 1M words by his count)
- **Low bar, no word count [S]:**
  - Commit to "an introduction, one paragraph, or ten minutes."
  - "Don't aim for word count, aim for consistency."
  - Avoid goals like "1000 words a day"; "strive for writing one sentence."
  - "I don't have a writing target. I just want to make sure I write something daily."
  - Sources: [dariusforoux.com: Form A Daily Writing Habit](https://dariusforoux.com/daily-writing-habit/); [What I Learned From Writing 600 Articles & 8 Books](https://dariusforoux.com/learned-from-writing/)
- **Timer ritual [S]:** "Use a timer. Set 25–30 minutes. No distractions. No editing. Just write." Finish and publish even if the piece isn't perfect. Don't overthink the editor app. — [dariusforoux.com](https://dariusforoux.com/daily-writing-habit/); [Lessons From 9 Years of Writing Daily](https://dariusforoux.medium.com/lessons-i-learned-from-9-years-of-writing-daily-1d22293bf2e8)
- **Editing tests [S]:**
  - "Stop over-editing before you've even finished a draft."
  - "Reading builds taste. Taste guides your edits."
  - Good writing is "clear, credible, and persuasive." Readers shouldn't think "What does this person mean?"
  - He cut a book draft from about 75K to 50K words.
  - "Always… question the necessity of each sentence."
  - Sources: [The Pursuit of Good Writing](https://dariusforoux.com/the-pursuit-of-good-writing/); [Foroux LinkedIn: Effective Writing](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/dariusforoux_effective-writing-by-darius-foroux-activity-7159633158714347520-PALH)
- **2026 [S]:** back to daily writing, framed as thinking and craft rather than a productivity tactic: "Pick a time and place that you can return to." Daily writing can mean asking questions, organizing notes, research or editing. — [Wise & Wealthy: Why I'm back to daily writing](https://wisewealthy.dariusforoux.com/p/why-im-back-to-daily-writing); [Writing every day will (still) change your life](https://wisewealthy.dariusforoux.com/p/writing-every-day-will-still-change)

#### Sinem Günel (Medium Writing Academy; Substack "Write Build Scale")
- **Ideal Reader Persona [N, Substack, date unknown, clipped 2025-11-05]:**
  - "Jennifer is one of several reader personas I write for."
  - Persona questions cover demographics, background and beliefs, goals ("why does she need your advice?") and challenges and fears ("Which problems could you help her solve…? Educational stories? Step-by-step guides? Or just inspiring/funny stories…?").
  - "When you know who you write for… you're able to come up with stronger arguments to clearly articulate the value of your story."
  - Source: [writebuildscale.substack.com: How to Build an "Ideal Reader Persona"](https://writebuildscale.substack.com/p/how-to-build-ideal-reader-persona), via [GitHub Readwise note](https://github.com/faculorca/thenotegarden)
- **Headlines [S, podcast]:** her early headlines were "really bad," and headlines are the main theme of common beginner mistakes. — [Writerly Lifestyle Ep. 11](https://writerlylifestyle.com/sinem-gunel-medium-writing/)
- **Volume vs. quality [S]:**
  - She advises writing quality articles rather than "loads of content just for the hell of it."
  - She wrote 150+ articles over 1.5 years without understanding audience-building.
  - She later reported 300k monthly views and more than $7k a month (date unclear).
  - Sources: [Sam Pennsimkins: Online Writing Challenge Day 4](https://medium.com/@sam.pennsimkins/what-i-learned-during-the-online-writing-challenge-by-sinem-gunel-day-4-5dc085813121); [Better Marketing: You Don't Need to be a Professional Writer](https://medium.com/better-marketing/you-dont-need-to-be-a-professional-writer-to-succeed-on-medium-8334ba2c6377)
- **Named course [S]:** "Medium Writing Academy." Her process is for nonfiction, and she says she doesn't have "the authority to say my process would be equally valuable for a different kind of writing." — [Günel reply](https://sinemgnel.medium.com/it-could-be-8fe1aa11ba2a)

#### Ali Abdaal (newsletter LifeNotes / Sunday Snippets; YouTube)
- **FBR = "Fast, Bad, Wrong" [S, his own newsletter]:**
  - The first draft accepts "terrible style, terrible grammar, terrible word choice, wrong facts" to keep the narrative moving. He edits later.
  - He learned it from Safi Bahcall on The Tim Ferriss Show, who describes three writing modes: **hunting, drafting and editing**.
  - He also uses it to plan videos.
  - An aggregator says "Fast, Bad, Right" and misspells the guest's name. Ali's own post says "Wrong."
  - Source: [aliabdaal.com: The acronym that changed how I write](https://aliabdaal.com/newsletter/the-acronym-that-changed-how-i-write/)
- **Feedback loop [S]:** reader replies to the weekly newsletter go straight into the following week's post. — [same](http://email.aliabdaal.com/issues/the-acronym-that-changed-how-i-write-188247); see also [Learn in Public, It's Great](https://aliabdaal.com/newsletter/learn-in-public-its-great/)
- **Scale [S]:** the newsletter grew "from 0 to 250,000 subscribers in the last 3…" years, per a LinkedIn post from about 2023. A later snapshot shows 230k+ on LifeNotes, so the figures conflict. — [Abdaal LinkedIn](https://lt.linkedin.com/posts/ali-abdaal_my-email-newsletter-has-grown-from-0-to-250000-activity-7042187590648651776-LGjc)
- **"Rule of 3" outlining:** a third party turned it into a ChatGPT prompt. This is not Ali's own AI use. — [Write With AI](https://writewithai.substack.com/p/outlining-with-ai-how-to-whip-up)

## Q2. Cross-creator principles, disagreements, and evidence vs. folklore

### Takeaway
There is broad agreement on four things:
1. Separate drafting from editing.
2. Write headline-first or structure-first.
3. Write for one specific reader.
4. Set a low daily bar, because consistency beats intensity.

Real disagreements exist on four points:
1. **Niche:** Denning says "you don't need a f*cking niche." Perell says create your own niche, a Personal Monopoly. Günel and Medium say define a persona.
2. **Volume vs. quality:** Denning batches 5 a day. Günel and Rane say quality, with Rane at 2–6 stories a month.
3. **Virality:** Perell says "things that go viral just are better." Medium says "don't try to go viral." Kuegler calls it "viral crap."
4. **Multi-platform:** Denning keeps several "racehorses," rewritten natively for each. Kuegler went all-in on one platform.

Almost all of it is survivor testimony. The only "evidence" offered is self-reported earnings and views.

### Inferences
- **The consensus stack:** ideas come from lived experience, an angle and one reader. Then headline and structure first, a fast ugly draft, a separate editing pass with defamiliarization (new font, new place, read aloud), and publish. The habit runs on a low daily minimum.
- The niche "disagreement" is mostly about wording. Everyone wants a *recognizable person and perspective*. They differ on whether to name a topic.
- The real split is **volume-era Medium (2016–2022)** vs. **post-2023 quality/Boost and AI-glut era**. Denning's batch volume and the 10-headlines drill belong to the first. The rule-breaking, emotion and "takes" advice belongs to the second.
- Folklore risk is highest for exact numbers (headlines per day, articles per batch, 480k words a month) and for platform mechanics like tags. Risk is lowest for process heuristics, which are low-cost to try.

### Gaps
- No controlled or quantitative evidence for any craft rule (headline count, sentence length, epiphany density).
- Couldn't verify whether Denning still batches in 2025–26 or has cut volume to one weekly Substack essay. The 2026 corpus shows weekly free essays, which suggests lower public volume. That is inference only.

## Q3. Which practices become agent checklists or prompts, and which must stay human

### Takeaway
Mechanical tests transfer well to an agent:
- headline clarity ("5th grader"; "why click?")
- top-of-page context, questions and one-sentence answer
- CRIBS feedback
- an epiphany every ~250 words
- exclamation-point, hedge, banned-word, cliché and paragraph-length checks
- TK markers
- the "is every sentence necessary" cut pass
- reader-persona Q&A
- generating 10 headline variants
- rewriting natively for each platform

What these writers themselves say AI *cannot* do must stay human: lived stories and the "bank of stories," a perspective or angle, emotion and vulnerability (including how much to reveal), the courage to state a take, taste and choosing which idea to bet on. Medium also makes it policy: paywalled stories must be human-written (see the earlier notes).

### Inferences

**Proposed agent pipeline, assembled from the sources above:**
1. **Interview the human** (past self, talk it out, email to a friend) and capture their stories, takes and receipts.
2. **Persona card** using Günel's questions.
3. **Top-of-page brief** in Perell's form: context, questions, one-sentence answer.
4. **Headline batch** of 10, scored on the 5th-grader test, "why click?" and Cole's what/who/promise (earlier notes).
5. **Outline** in Rule of 3 / Hardy's subpoints form.
6. **The human, or the agent clearly marked as such, does an FBR draft with TK markers.**
7. **Lint pass:** hedges, exclamation points, banned words and clichés, paragraph length, sentence-length variance, an epiphany every ~250 words, sentence necessity, and drift from the top-of-page answer.
8. **CRIBS self-review.**
9. **Platform-native rewrite** for X, LinkedIn or a Substack Note.

**Linter caveat (anti-AI-sameness):** Eliason and Denning warn that rule-perfect output reads as AI. The lint step should *flag* issues, not auto-fix them. It should also keep one or two deliberate rule-breaks the human chose.

**Hard-human items:**
- choosing the angle
- the confessional or vulnerable content, and how much to reveal
- any first-person "receipt" (money, views), which must be verified and true
- emotional register
- the final "would I put my name on this" call

Medium's paywall policy also requires human authorship for monetized stories, so agents should assist, not ghostwrite, for MPP.

- No writer here published a concrete AI-assisted workflow with prompts. The Rule of 3 prompt is a third party's.
- No evidence on whether agent-linted drafts perform better or worse on Medium, Boost or Substack.
