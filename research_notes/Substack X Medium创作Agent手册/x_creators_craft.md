## 1. Idea generation (content pillars, idea banks, atomic essays, recycling, trends)

### Takeaway
The main documented methods are: (a) put ideas in a matrix of topics × formats (Justin Welsh's Content Matrix; Ship 30's "4A" buckets), (b) keep a capture habit and a swipe file (Sahil Bloom's pocket notebook; Dan Koe's AI-assisted swipe file of top-performing posts), (c) test ideas as short posts on X first and expand the ones that work (Hormozi, Dan Koe), and (d) recycle proven winners on a schedule (Welsh's republish dates, Tim Denning revisiting topics). "Reply to trends" as a method is barely documented in the sources found.

### Cited Findings
- **Ship 30 "4A" framework** (Dickie Bush / Nicolas Cole): any topic can be written about in 4 ways: **Actionable** (how-to), **Analytical** (numbers-driven), **Aspirational** ("it's possible for you"), **Anthropological** (why things happen). Bush gives book examples, e.g. *The Psychology of Money* = Anthropological. Cole's "Digital Writing Tip #4" says "all content can be reverse-engineered in 4 buckets." Threads date from about 2021–2022. [P, via threadreader mirror] — [Bush thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1534574544895234049.html); [Cole thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1441450057119592456.html)
- **Atomic essay** (Ship 30 for 30): a short, self-contained piece of about 250–500 words that explores one idea. The program asks participants to publish one piece a day for 30 days. Length guidance differs by source (about 250 words or one printed page, vs. ≤500 words). [S] — [dsebastien concept note](https://concepts.dsebastien.net/concept/atomic-essays/); [kyoto.synaps atomic essays](https://kyoto.synaps.media/atomic-essays); Bush's "Atomic Essays Republishing Framework" thread (2021) — [threadreader](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1361689241978163202.html)
- **Justin Welsh Content Matrix**: a spreadsheet for brainstorming "hundreds of content ideas in one quick blast." Commonly described as X-axis = content types and Y-axis = topics, with each cell becoming a headline idea. [S/W; I found no primary Welsh page] — [Tweet Hunter blog](https://tweethunter.io/blog/justin-welsh-content-repurposing-system); [Ole Lehmann: ChatGPT for the Content Matrix](https://olelehmann.beehiiv.com/p/use-chatgpt-fill-justin-welshs-genius-content-matrix-seconds); [The Way Up](https://thewayup.beehiiv.com/p/how-justin-welsh-built-1-5m-followers-with-a-content-system)
- **Welsh "730-Day Content Library"**: a Notion database that calculates republish dates at 6, 12, 18 and 24 months, so winners get reused and weak posts get reworked. Ready posts go into Taplio (LinkedIn) or another tool (X). [W, summary of an article I could not open] — [marketnews.xskill summary](https://marketnews.xskill.com/a/5138?m=en)
- **Sahil Bloom capture habit**: a dot-grid Moleskine pocket notebook and a Fisher Space Pen, plus a weekly Sunday 1-hour walk with only the notebook. [S] — [Practical PKM "Creativity Flywheels Vol. 2"](https://practicalpkm.kit.com/posts/creativity-flywheels-vol-2-sahil-bloom). He sorts threads into **evergreen vs. timely** (from a Nathan Barry / Creator Science podcast summary) and runs the topic, hook and structure past close friends before publishing. [S] — [Creator Science podcast](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/sahil-bloom/); [Tweet Hunter thread-finder "12 growth lessons from Sahil Bloom"](https://tweethunter.io/thread-finder/tibo_maker/12-twitter-growth-lessons-from-sahil-bloom)
- **Dan Koe swipe file + AI ideation** (Greg Isenberg, Startup Ideas Podcast, Oct 2025): uses X for idea validation, keeps a swipe file, and uses AI (Claude/ChatGPT) to analyze high-performing content ("content deconstruction") and generate new ideas. He recommends using Tweet Hunter X or SuperX to pull the top-performing tweets of accounts you admire. [S/W recaps] — [Recapio digest](https://recapio.com/digest/i-watched-dan-koe-break-down-his-ai-workflow-omg-by-greg-isenberg); [Stormy.ai](https://stormy.ai/blog/dan-koe-ai-workflow-viral-ideation-engine); [woshipm 中文解析](https://www.woshipm.com/ai/6283902.html); Isenberg's guide at gregisenberg.com/content-engine (not fetched)
- **Hormozi: X as a test bed**: test ideas on Twitter/TikTok, see what resonates, then expand. His team reportedly publishes about 300 pieces a week. He is quoted saying "if the source of your content is other people's content, don't make it" (an originality rule, not "document don't create"; that exact phrase was **not** found attributed to him). [S/W] — [Video Creatr podcast "How Alex Hormozi posts 300+ times a week"](https://www.buzzsprout.com/1887347/episodes/17152925-how-alex-hormozi-posts-300-times-a-week); [Deciphr keynote summary](https://www.deciphr.ai/podcast/lets-talk-content-creation-keynote-pt1-ep-402); [Recapio/Castmagic](https://recapio.com/digest/alex-hormozis-advice-on-content-creation-by-castmagic)
- **Tim Denning: recycling**: his Medium success is attributed to revisiting his own topics from new angles rather than to volume alone. [S, no first-person source] — [byburk "secret of Tim Denning's success on Medium"](https://letters.byburk.net/p/this-is-the-secret-of-tim-dennings-success-on-medium)
- **Naval Ravikant**: the 2018 "How to Get Rich (without getting lucky)" tweetstorm compressed principles he said he had carried for about 30 years (since age 13–14). Each tweet "can be extrapolated into an hour's worth of conversation." So the ideation is lived experience distilled into aphorisms, not a content calendar. [S] — [Medium notes from Naval's Periscope, 6/6/2018](https://medium.com/@DayanaBreathe/naval-ravikant-notes-from-periscope-on-his-tweet-storm-how-to-get-rich-without-getting-lucky-e9e4f03a955f); The Almanack (Eric Jorgenson) was compiled from tweets, podcasts and interviews — [EJorgenson.com](https://EJorgenson.com/books-1)
- **Jack Butcher / Visualize Value**: started as a daily visual-content brand on Twitter and Instagram. The idea for his course came from audience demand: David Perell tweeted that he wanted to learn to design like VV, Butcher polled his audience, and the response led to the courses. [P/S] — [Visualize Value blog "Build Once, Sell Twice"](https://visualizevalue.com/blog/brain-dumps-build-once-sell-twice); [Callum McDonnell](https://callummcdonnell.substack.com/p/how-jack-butcher-built-visualise)
- **Pieter Levels**: build-in-public since about 2013. Content comes from the work itself: revenue numbers, launches, the "12 startups in 12 months" challenge that produced Nomad List and Remote OK. [S] — [SoftwareSeni](https://www.softwareseni.com/building-in-public-the-10-year-distribution-strategy-behind-solo-founder-revenue/); [Favikon](https://favikon.com/blog/who-is-levelsio)
- **Chinese-language X (中文推特)**: one practitioner post says they reached about 10K followers in about 30 days by posting daily, moving notes already proven on Xiaohongshu (小红书) to X, and posting only long tweets (no Articles). They advise keeping a buffer of 20–30 topics. [W] — [YouMind X growth guide](https://youmind.com/ko-KR/landing/x-viral-articles/x-twitter-growth-guide). An analysis of 23 top Chinese-X AI creators (2 months, posts >10K views) found **none of the 17 posts over 1M views were about AI**; life and humor content drew more traffic. Template titles like "2026 + 完整指南" are common. The author warns the sample is small. — [KuCoin News flash](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/analysis-of-23-top-ai-creators-on-chinese-x-ai-content-lags-behind-viral-posts)

### Inferences
- Two ideation systems recur: **combinatorial** (topic × format grids: Welsh matrix, 4A) and **empirical** (post short, watch metrics, expand the winners: Hormozi, Koe, Welsh recycling). An agent could run both: generate from the grid, then rank by past engagement.
- Builder-creators (Levels, Karpathy, Butcher) get material from their own work, not from content systems. Their "idea bank" is their work log.
- Cross-platform arbitrage (moving content proven on 小红书, LinkedIn or Medium to X) shows up in both English and Chinese sources.

- No primary Welsh page for the exact matrix layout could be fetched (justinwelsh.me blocked).
- I found no documented method for "reply to trends / newsjacking" from these named creators.
- Andrej Karpathy, Codie Sanchez, Lenny Rachitsky, Packy McCormick and Shaan Puri's ideation: searches returned nothing usable (Karpathy returned zero hits). Not researched further because of the tool budget.
- George Mack: no source found for his process. I could not confirm he has been a guest on Perell's How I Write.

## 2. Writing templates and frameworks (posts, threads, X Articles)

### Takeaway
The best-documented frameworks are Ship 30's **1-3-1** micro-structure and the 4A angle framework; Dan Koe's **hook → "aha" insight → practical process** outline; Shaan Puri's **"write like you talk"** with talk-first drafting, copywork and an 8th-grade reading level; and Paul Graham's view of writing as thinking followed by heavy simplifying edits. For X Articles (long-form, launched Mar 2024, opened to all Premium in Jan 2026) I found no documented creator-specific templates, only platform news and weak "playbook" blogs.

### Cited Findings
- **Ship 30 "1-3-1"**: one practitioner reports the formula taught by Nicolas Cole in a Ship 30 cohort as "1 line opener, 3 line explainer and one line wrap up." [S, one secondhand blog; the official wording was not verified] — [evchapman.com](https://evchapman.com/blog/four-tools-every-creator-needs-to-ship-creative-work-at-pace)
- **Cole's thread lead-in tweet**: the first tweet should share a Story or a Framework, or hint at the Actionable Advice to come. Headlines should be "clear instead of clever." [P via mirror] — [Cole thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1441450057119592456.html)
- **Dan Koe outline** (newsletter, which then feeds posts): (1) a hook built on a relatable problem, (2) a novel "aha" idea, (3) practical tips or a step-by-step solution. He spends 1–2 days gathering thoughts and outlining before drafting. [S] — [beehiiv case study "How Dan Koe Makes $2M/yr Writing 2 Hours per Day"](https://www.beehiiv.com/case-studies/dan-koe) (snippet only; fetch blocked); [tagabukid Substack](https://tagabukid.substack.com/p/how-dan-koe-writes-one-newsletter)
- **Shaan Puri**: "write like you talk" is one of his biggest rules (said on How I Write). When stuck, speak out loud, write down what you said, then cut unnecessary words. Copywork (hand-copying writing you admire) is "the cure for writer's block." Pick the emotion you want readers to feel before writing. Aim for about an 8th-grade reading level. The MFM "Become a Better Writer in 60 Minutes" masterclass (with Sam Parr) includes a "voice memo test" and a three-step framework. [P/S] — [Maven "Shaan Puri's Writing Tips"](https://maven.com/articles/shaan-puri-writing); [MFM masterclass page](https://www.mfmpod.com/become-a-better-writer-in-60-minutes-masterclass/); [Shaan thread mirror](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1489012995388628998.html)
- **Paul Graham**: writing is how he thinks; good writers discover ideas while drafting. He writes a first draft, then spends hours or days editing, and much of the editing is simplifying. "Write as simply as you can." [S, secondary notes on PG essays] — [Adarsh Badri "Paul Graham on writing well"](https://fuzzynotes.adarshbadri.me/p/paul-graham-on-writing-well); [dsebastien PG advice notes](https://notes.dsebastien.net/30+Areas/32+Literature+notes/32.02+Content/Paul+Graham's+Advice+for+Writers)
- **Naval's form**: aphoristic tweetstorm. Each line is a self-contained principle that can be expanded (see §1). [S] — [Medium Periscope notes](https://medium.com/@DayanaBreathe/naval-ravikant-notes-from-periscope-on-his-tweet-storm-how-to-get-rich-without-getting-lucky-e9e4f03a955f)
- **Sahil Bloom thread length**: "7–12 tweets" is cited as his sweet spot by a third-party style-guide site. [W, not from Bloom] — [autotweet.io style guide](https://www.autotweet.io/creators/sahil-bloom-style). He is widely credited with popularizing explainer threads that simplify complex (finance) ideas into evergreen, bookmarkable form. [S] — [Growing Viral](https://growingviral.beehiiv.com/p/sahil-bloom-corporate-finance-1m-follower-creator)
- **Visualize Value**: a minimalist visual (black and white, a diagram plus a short line) is the post itself. Butcher sells "How to Visualize Value" as a playbook for this. [P] — [visualizevalue.com/about](https://www.visualizevalue.com/about)
- **X Articles platform facts**: launched March 2024 for Premium+. Opened to all Premium subscribers in Jan 2026. X offered a **$1M prize** for the most popular Article published by Jan 30, 2026. Musk argued long-form "accumulate[s] more user seconds," which raises its algorithmic weight. A Grok-powered audio option was added. [S news] — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/amp/digital/x-expands-long-form-articles-access-to-all-premium-subscribers-87544.htm); [PiunikaWeb, 2026-01-07](https://piunikaweb.com/2026/01/07/x-articles-premium-nikita-bier-updates/); [Siècle Digital, 2026-01-08](https://siecledigital.fr/2026/01/08/x-mise-sur-les-articles-longs-pour-reconquerir-journalistes-et-createurs/); [Pulse/Grok audio](https://www.pulse.bot/ai/news/x-adds-grok-powered-audio-option-to-long-form-articles-5a8a3117-755b-4c57-a453-6094ea9c0ec7)
- **Reported Articles surge**: an "18×" surge in long-form publishing after the prize and subscription changes (Social Media Today, Mar 2026, relayed by Pulse). [W, not verified against the original] — [Pulse](https://www.pulse.bot/digital-marketing/news/x-announces-1-million-prize-for-top-x-article-this-month-620a89f1-bec9-4a94-9bd7-1fd8b84c4b49). A claim that Articles get extra weight in creator-payout calculations comes from one unsourced blog. — [Socialync](https://www.socialync.io/blog/x-twitter-articles-strategy-2026)
- **Chinese long-post craft** (经验帖): plain language without jargon, everyday analogies for complex ideas, personal experience and real feelings. Long form is used to build expert status, with WeChat 公众号 viral articles (爆文) as the model. One secondary article says the algorithm favors information-dense, logically argued content and suggests titles that pair a counter-intuitive claim with a specific number. [W] — [YouMind guide](https://youmind.com/ko-KR/landing/x-viral-articles/x-twitter-growth-guide); [KuCoin "X's new algorithm reshapes content ecosystem"](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/x-s-new-algorithm-reshapes-content-ecosystem-by-2026)

### Inferences
- A workable stack for an agent: angle from the 4A buckets → hook (Cole: story, framework or promise; "clear not clever") → 1-3-1 body for single posts, or a hook / aha / steps outline (Koe) for threads, Articles and newsletters → a "read aloud / voice-memo" pass (Puri) → a simplification pass (PG).
- Formatting (line breaks, whitespace, one idea per line) is implied by 1-3-1 but I found no measured data on it.

### Gaps
- Official Ship 30 wording of 1-3-1 and the "three types of threads that work" (mentioned in a webinar promo, but not listed in the snippets) were not verified.
- No creator-authored template for X Articles was found. Most "Articles playbooks" are tool-vendor SEO pages.
- Hook-formula catalogs from Typefully/Hypefury blogs were not retrieved.

## 3. Production workflow (batching, scheduling tools, time spent, drafting, AI)

### Takeaway
The common pattern is a fixed daily writing block of about 2 hours (Koe, Welsh), weekly batching of short posts, one long piece per week as the "hub," schedulers (Taplio for LinkedIn; Typefully/Hypefury-type tools for X), and AI used for analysis and ideation more than for drafting (Koe). Hormozi is the high-volume exception: a team cuts long-form recordings into many clips.

### Cited Findings
- **Dan Koe**: one weekly newsletter powers everything. A daily morning 2-hour block covers one newsletter section plus about 3 social posts. Newsletters become YouTube scripts. 2–3 social posts a day. AI is used for summarizing, ideation, finding links between his own past pieces, and refining. He does **not** have it write first drafts (per recaps). [S/W] — [beehiiv case study (snippet)](https://www.beehiiv.com/case-studies/dan-koe); [Recapio](https://recapio.com/digest/i-watched-dan-koe-break-down-his-ai-workflow-omg-by-greg-isenberg); [Koe-cast "2 Hour Morning Routine"](https://pocketcasts.com/podcast/the-koe-cast/45f95490-82f5-0139-34c5-0acc26574db2/the-2-hour-morning-routine-that-has-made-me-10-million/5256fffd-4a91-4cf3-9299-1bf2d55a92da)
- **Community reproductions of Koe's AI system**: Greg Isenberg's downloadable guide (gregisenberg.com/content-engine) and an open-source "AI Content Engine" that runs on **Claude Code** as slash commands, including a swipe-file generator that pulls hooks, patterns and psychological triggers from URLs. [P for repo existence] — [AI Content Engine README (jsDelivr)](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/vincentchan/ai-content-engine@main/README.md); [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/posts/ai-content-engine-dan-koe-inspired)
- **Justin Welsh weekly calendar** (as reported): daily LinkedIn post (about 7:15 AM) plus X replies; Monday thread brainstorming; Tuesday newsletter draft; Wednesday batch of about 10 LinkedIn/X posts. About 2 hours a day, a Sunday weekly review, batching by energy level, Notion for documentation. Business software cost about $620/mo. A "75-minute newsletter writing process" is also referenced. [W, aggregator pages] — [raydata vault: Content OS 2.0, 2026-04-06](https://hq-raydata-co.pages.dev/vault/06-reference/2026-04-06-content-os-welsh/); [raydata vault: 75-min newsletter, 2026-04-03](https://hq-raydata-co.pages.dev/vault/06-reference/2026-04-03-justin-welsh-newsletter-writing-process/); [Kajabi hero story](https://www.kajabi.com/blog/hero-story-justin-welsh)
- **Welsh hub-and-spoke**: one long piece becomes 6–10 short "spoke" posts plus 2 CTAs that move followers off-platform. Example: an X thread and LinkedIn post about earning $150K/month were turned into that week's newsletter. LinkedIn is the primary platform and X the repurpose platform (per a third-party profile). [S] — [Growing Viral](https://growingviral.beehiiv.com/p/justin-welsh-turning-1-piece-content-100-highvalue-posts); [entrepreneur.com](https://entrepreneur.com/growing-a-business/justin-welsh-shares-a-4-step-strategy-for-monetizing-your/437017); [autotweet style guide](https://www.autotweet.io/creators/justin-welsh-style)
- **Hormozi**: about 300 posts a week. A five-phase "content machine" moves from simply posting to capturing content across platforms. A podcast hour can produce about 50–70 clips (German digest). **Tension:** his 2026 strategy summary favors live, interactive content and long-form video over short-form. [S/W] — [Video Creatr podcast](https://www.buzzsprout.com/1887347/episodes/17152925-how-alex-hormozi-posts-300-times-a-week); [lilys.ai (DE)](https://lilys.ai/de/notes/solopreneur-20251214/alex-hormozi-content-creation-advice); [Rosetta "My Actual Social Media Strategy For 2026"](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/my-actual-social-media-strategy-for-2026)
- **Tim Denning**: reportedly posts daily on LinkedIn, X and Medium/newsletter, with a different strategy for each. SuperX says he repurposes top threads into short videos and LinkedIn posts. [W] — [SuperX profile](https://superx.so/creators/Tim_Denning); [Marketing Speak podcast](https://www.marketingspeak.com/?p=9699)
- **Scheduling tools**: Welsh uses Taplio for LinkedIn (per the summary above). I found no snippet confirming which named creator uses Typefully, Hypefury or Tweet Hunter for X, apart from Koe recommending Tweet Hunter X / SuperX for research. — [Stormy.ai](https://stormy.ai/blog/dan-koe-ai-workflow-viral-ideation-engine)

### Inferences
- "2 hours a day" is the recurring time budget in solo-creator stories (Koe, Welsh). It is self-reported and works partly as a marketing claim for their courses (Koe's "2 Hour Writer," Welsh's "Content OS").
- The workflow common to most of these creators is: capture daily → outline the weekly hub → 2h writing block → batch and schedule spokes → reply and engage live → recycle winners on a 6–12 month cycle.

### Gaps
- Typefully and Hypefury blogs could not be fetched. I have no first-hand tool-usage confirmations for Bloom, Cole/Bush, Koe or Hormozi.
- Karpathy, Levels and Naval clearly do not run batch/schedule systems, but there are no sources to document this; it is inferred from their style.

## 4. X → newsletter funnel and how X content relates to long-form

### Takeaway
Every case found works on the same model: X is the discovery and testing layer, and email (beehiiv, Substack, Kit) is where the audience is owned and monetized. Long-form is either the source that short posts are cut from (Koe, Welsh) or the expansion of short posts that worked (Hormozi, Naval → Almanack). Bloom's subscriber growth is reported as driven by long Twitter threads.

### Cited Findings
- **Sahil Bloom**: about 500 followers in May 2020 → about 187K by the end of 2021 → about 580K in 2022 (later figures of about 700K and 930K are cited by other sources). The Curiosity Chronicle launched in 2021 and reportedly reached 134K subscribers in about 1.5 years, about 300K by mid-2022, and 800K+ in recent listings. Its growth is attributed to long, informative threads. A Kit case study covers "47,000 subscribers in 6 weeks." [S; figures vary by source and date] — [Growing Viral](https://growingviral.beehiiv.com/p/sahil-bloom-corporate-finance-1m-follower-creator); [Kit/Matt "47,000 in 6 weeks"](https://matt.kit.com/posts/how-sahil-bloom-added-47-000-subscribers-in-6-weeks); [entrepreneur.com "$70K newsletter"](https://entrepreneur.com/starting-a-business/how-sahil-bloom-built-a-newsletter-that-makes-70000-a/467228); [Substack](https://sahilbloom.substack.com)
- **Dan Koe**: beehiiv case study titled "How Dan Koe Makes $2 Million per Year Writing 2 Hours per Day." The newsletter is the core output, and X posts are derived from it and used to validate ideas. [S/P-ish, beehiiv's own case study, snippet only] — [beehiiv](https://www.beehiiv.com/case-studies/dan-koe)
- **Justin Welsh**: the weekly Saturday essay is cited at about 180K readers. Spokes include 2 CTAs that move followers off-platform. [W] — [Growing Viral](https://growingviral.beehiiv.com/p/justin-welsh-turning-1-piece-content-100-highvalue-posts)
- **Jack Butcher**: Twitter/IG visuals → audience poll → courses (How to Visualize Value; Build Once, Sell Twice, now 55 lessons) and a membership ($9/mo; BOST $99/yr). Revenue claims vary ($180K/mo peak; "~$1M/yr"; unverified). [P for pricing, S for revenue] — [Visualize Value](https://www.visualizevalue.com/about); [Everand podcast listing](https://www.everand.com/podcast/590711323/041-Jack-Butcher-Build-Once-Sell-Twice-Earn-1M-a-Year-Selling-Digital-Products-Jack-Butcher-is-the-founder-of-Visualize-Value-a-design-consul)
- **Naval**: tweetstorm (2018) → podcast series expanding each tweet → *The Almanack of Naval Ravikant* (Jorgenson compiled it from tweets, podcasts and interviews). This is the clearest case of short posts later assembled into long-form. [S] — [EJorgenson.com](https://EJorgenson.com/books-1)
- **Pieter Levels**: here the funnel goes to products, not a newsletter. Public revenue and launch updates on X drive product sign-ups. [S] — [SoftwareSeni](https://www.softwareseni.com/building-in-public-the-10-year-distribution-strategy-behind-solo-founder-revenue/)
- **Hormozi**: "adding calls to action" is one step in his five-step growth process. [W] — [Deciphr](https://www.deciphr.ai/podcast/how-i-gained-78-million-followers-in-40-months-6-key-lessons)

### Inferences
- There are two directions: **long → short** (Koe, Welsh: write the weekly hub, then cut posts from it) and **short → long** (Naval, Hormozi, Bloom: post short, then expand the posts that worked). Ship 30's atomic essays sit in between: test with an atomic essay, then expand to long-form.
- X Articles (2026) may shrink the need to leave X for long-form, but no creator case study in the sources shows Articles replacing a newsletter.

### Gaps
- No primary data on X→email conversion rates (e.g., subscribers per 1K impressions).
- Lenny Rachitsky's and Packy McCormick's X use, and Codie Sanchez's funnel, were not documented in the snippets retrieved.

## 5. Substantiated (data) vs. folklore

### Takeaway
Almost none of the tactical advice (thread length, 1-3-1, posting times, "threads beat single posts") rests on public, rigorous data. The only hard platform facts are X's open-sourced ranking code (2023 Heavy Ranker; Jan 2026 Grok-based "Phoenix" transformer, updated May 2026) and the Articles access and prize announcements. Creator follower, subscriber and revenue numbers are self-reported and differ across sources.

### Cited Findings
- **Algorithm**: X open-sourced its "For You" algorithm again in Jan 2026 (Apache-2.0). It is a Grok-based transformer ("Phoenix") that predicts engagement probabilities from users' historical action sequences, replacing the 2023 Heavy/Light ranker that used hand-engineered features. A May 15, 2026 update added an end-to-end pipeline, a downloadable Phoenix model and an ads module. ### Inferences
- **Substantiated** (platform-documented): the algorithm is engagement-prediction-based (so replies, dwell time and engagement history matter in principle); Articles exist with broad Premium access; the $1M prize was real.
- **Plausible but self-reported** (survivorship bias): the 2-hour/day workflows, the matrix and 4A brainstorming, recycling winners, and the hub-and-spoke model. These are consistent across creators but mostly sold as courses (Ship 30, Welsh's Content OS, Koe's 2 Hour Writer, BOST), so the incentive is to make the system look simple.
- **Folklore / unverified**: specific thread lengths (7–12), "threads beat singles by X%," specific posting times, "links are penalized" (no snippet showed this in the 2026 code), Articles payout boosts.
