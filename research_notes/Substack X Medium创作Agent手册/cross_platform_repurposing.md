# Cross-Platform Content Repurposing & Multi-Channel Distribution (Substack / X / Medium / LinkedIn / Threads)

Research date: 2026-11-03. Method note: about 19 web searches/fetches. **Direct page fetches were blocked by the session network sandbox for buffer.com and typefully.com / support.typefully.com.** So nearly every claim below comes from search-engine result snippets or summaries of the pages, not from full-page reads. Treat everything as "snippet-level" unless it says otherwise. Many sources are vendor or creator-marketing blogs (beehiiv, Tweet Hunter, OpenTweet, Narrareach, and others) that have a commercial interest. That is flagged where it matters.

## 1. Repurposing frameworks and real creator workflows (direction of flow and why)

### Takeaway
The documented creator systems mostly run **long→short (hub-and-spoke)**: a weekly newsletter or pillar video is the "source of truth", and it is cut into 6–30 social micro-pieces that point back to it (Welsh, Koe, Gary Vee, Sahil Bloom as described by beehiiv, Lenny). There is also a strong **short→long feedback loop**: X/LinkedIn posts serve as cheap idea tests, and the winners get expanded into newsletters (Koe; Ship 30's atomic essays→newsletters/books). The practical model is a cycle: test short, expand the winners long, then atomize the long piece again.

### Cited Findings
**Justin Welsh – "Content Operating System" (LinkedIn/X → newsletter)**
- Official course page: a multi-step system for producing a high-quality newsletter plus 6–12 pieces of high-performance social content each week. About 90-minute video course, listed at $79 (down from $200). — [learn.justinwelsh.me/content](https://learn.justinwelsh.me/content); [learn.justinwelsh.me/the-content-os](https://learn.justinwelsh.me/the-content-os); a Coda version exists at [coda.io/@justin-welsh/the-content-operating-system](https://coda.io/@justin-welsh/the-content-operating-system)
- Third-party breakdowns describe the newsletter as the weekly "source of truth", with one long-form "hub" turned into 6–10 short "spokes" (thread, several tweets, LinkedIn posts, short video). — [Growing Viral (beehiiv)](https://growingviral.beehiiv.com/p/justin-welsh-turning-1-piece-content-100-highvalue-posts); [The Way Up](https://thewayup.beehiiv.com/p/how-justin-welsh-built-1-5m-followers-with-a-content-system)

- Third-party: he posts key-takeaway summaries and threads per newsletter topic on X, and similar content on LinkedIn while tagging company pages. **Gary Vaynerchuk – "reverse pyramid"**
- Gary's own essay: the strategy is "predicated on a 'reverse pyramid' model" that starts from pillar content (daily vlog, keynote, Q&A show), which his team cuts into platform-specific micro-content that drives people back to the pillar. — [garyvaynerchuk.com – The GaryVee Content Strategy](https://garyvaynerchuk.com/THE-GARYVEE-CONTENT-STRATEGY-HOW-TO-GROW-AND-DISTRIBUTE-YOUR-BRANDS-SOCIAL-MEDIA-CONTENT/) (snippet only)
- Timing heuristic from a third party: post the pillar on all platforms at 12:00, micro-content at 12:01, and run a second distribution round later. — [getgist.com](https://getgist.com/gary-vee-content-model/); [biteable](https://biteable.com/blog/gary-vees-content-framework-one-video-30-content-pieces/)

### Inferences
- **Long→short is dominant for monetized newsletter operators** because the newsletter is the owned, monetizable asset. Social posts act as acquisition and must point back to it. **Short→long is the R&D layer**: social engagement is a cheap signal for choosing which ideas earn a 2,000-word essay (Koe, Ship 30).
- A creator agent should support both directions: (a) "atomize" a long post into N platform-native posts; (b) "promote" a high-performing short post into a long-form outline. It should also have (c) an evergreen recycler with a minimum gap of about 30–90 days (Welsh, Hypefury).

### Gaps
- No primary-source step-by-step workflow from Denning, Welsh (beyond course marketing) or Bloom was retrieved. Most details are secondary.
- Ship 30 / Typeshare's current product status in 2026 was not verified.

## 2. Platform-specific adaptation rules (length, tone, links, canonical/SEO, timing)

### Takeaway
The hard rules are: (1) on X, **put the link in a reply or rely on Premium**, because non-Premium link posts were measured at near-zero median engagement in 2025, even after X said in October 2025 that it had dropped the penalty; (2) on Medium, **publish on Substack or your own site first, then use Medium's Import tool**, which sets the canonical automatically (Substack itself cannot set a canonical pointing elsewhere); (3) adapt the content natively and add a CTA per platform rather than pasting a bare link.

### Cited Findings
**X link handling**
- Buffer analysed 18.8M posts (published October 14, 2025): since March 2025, link posts from **non-Premium** accounts had median engagement of **zero**. Premium accounts still get reach on link posts, though below other formats. Snippet only; buffer.com was blocked from fetching. — [Buffer: Do posts with links affect performance on X?](https://buffer.com/resources/links-on-x/)
- A January 2025 secondary report says Musk confirmed the algorithm deprioritizes external-link posts and suggested putting links in replies. Original statement not located. — [hashmeta](https://hashmeta.com/insights/twitter-algorithm-changes-2025)
- X reportedly announced on October 14, 2025 that it had removed the "link penalty". 2026 blogs say suppression continues indirectly, because ranking optimizes on-platform dwell time and engagement. — [SocialPilot (Aug 2026)](https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/twitter-algorithm); [adlibrary](https://adlibrary.com/guides/x-twitter-algorithm-explained); [socialspool](https://socialspool.com/blog/x-algorithm-2026-reach-factors). Reach-loss figures (30–50%, 50–90%) repeated in these blogs are unverified.
- Precedent of targeted suppression: on April 6–9, 2023, right after Substack launched Notes (April 5), tweets with substack.com links could not be liked, retweeted or replied to, and links were flagged "unsafe". Custom domains and shorteners were unaffected. Restrictions were lifted by about April 8–9, but "Substack" searches redirected to "newsletter". — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/twitter-stops-throttling-tweets-with-substack-links-171858782.html); [MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/2023/04/07/substack-founders-respond-to-twitters-restrictions-on-tweets-with-links-to-competing-platform/); [iPhone in Canada](https://www.iphoneincanada.ca/2023/04/10/substack-supression-twitter); The Verge via mirror [kompas.wasmer.app/…theverge…](https://kompas.wasmer.app/https_www_theverge_com/2023/4/7/23674936/twitter-marking-substack-links-unsafe)
- Converting X followers into subscribers "requires more than tweeting a link" because the algorithm keeps users on-platform. The usual pattern is a value-complete thread with a plug in a reply. — [Skio interview with Sahil Bloom](https://skio.com/interviews/sahil-bloom-on-content-strategy-brand-voice-and-audience-growth); [beehiiv](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/how-to-turn-any-newsletter-into-a-viral-x-thread)

**Medium canonical / duplicate content**
- Medium Help: Medium's official cross-posting tools (Import) automatically set the imported source as canonical. Authors can also set it manually under "Customize canonical link", and only the story's author can set it. — [Medium Help: Set a canonical link](https://help.medium.com/hc/en-us/articles/360033930293-Set-a-canonical-link)
- Secondary: pasting content into Medium's editor manually does not carry a canonical. The Import tool can fail to parse some pages. Google sometimes indexes the Medium copy first, so staggering publication helps. — [byburk: cross-post without hurting SEO](https://blog.byburk.net/how-to-cross-post-without-hurting-your-seo); [victorious.com](https://victorious.com/blog/medium-canonical/)
- **Substack does not let writers set a canonical** pointing elsewhere. The standard topology is Substack (or own site) as the original, with Medium canonical → Substack. — [anshulkumar Substack](https://anshulkumar.substack.com/p/can-you-post-the-same-blog-on-substack); [startuphub canonical guide](https://www.startuphub.ai/news/canonical-cross-posting-guide)
- Conflict: one source says Medium's API supported canonicalUrl on push; another says canonical only works when Medium fetches a URL. Unresolved (and moot for new users; see §4). — [startuphub](https://www.startuphub.ai/ai-news/ai-tools/2026/auto-publish-devto-medium-cross-posting) vs [startuphub guide](https://www.startuphub.ai/ai-news/ai-tools/2026/canonical-cross-posting-guide.md)
- Medium→Substack funnel tactics (link back to the Substack in each Medium story, a "full version on Substack" CTA). — [byburk: turn Medium readers into Substack subscribers](https://letters.byburk.net/p/how-to-turn-medium-readers-into-substack); [pubstacksuccess: why I link to Medium posts in Substack](https://pubstacksuccess.substack.com/p/why-i-link-to-medium-posts-in-substack)

**LinkedIn / Threads / tone**
- Writers report lightly editing Substack/Medium pieces "to be more professional-sounding" before pasting into LinkedIn. — [Online Writing Club comment](https://www.onlinewritingclub.com/p/how-to-start-writing-on-substack/comment/41822466)
- LinkedIn→Substack conversion case study (3,000+ followers converted; self-reported). — [escapethecubicle](https://escapethecubicle.substack.com/p/the-linkedin-substack-flywheel-20/comments)
- Substack→LinkedIn repurposing guides. — [mattgiaro.com](https://mattgiaro.com/?p=16908)

**Substack Notes**
- Substack (July 2024, cited secondhand): writers who post 3+ thoughtful Notes in their launch week gain 50% more total subscribers. — [writestack.io](https://www.writestack.io/blog/how-to-grow-a-substack)
- Typefully can publish to Substack Notes (changelog title "Substack Notes, videos, mentions"; contents not fetched). — [Typefully changelog](https://typefully.com/changelog/substack-notes-videos-mentions)

**Timing**
- Gary Vee pattern: pillar first, micro-content immediately after, a second round later. — [getgist](https://getgist.com/gary-vee-content-model/)
- Welsh / Hypefury pattern: recycle evergreen posts after a 30–90 day gap. — [Tweet Hunter](https://tweethunter.io/blog/justin-welsh-content-repurposing-system); [makerstack Hypefury review](https://makerstack.co/reviews/hypefury-review/)
- Medium: stagger after the original is indexed. — [byburk](https://blog.byburk.net/how-to-cross-post-without-hurting-your-seo)

### Inferences
- Agent rules: X main post has no link (non-Premium) or is a link-in-first-reply; thread length 5–12 posts, with a closing post that quotes the hook and carries the CTA. LinkedIn gets a more professional tone and the link in a comment or at the end. Medium gets an import from the Substack URL 1–7 days later, with a canonical check after import. Substack Notes get 1–3 short Notes per post (restack plus a pull quote).
- Avoid raw substack.com URLs on X if possible (use a custom domain) given the 2023 targeting precedent. This is an inference from a single historic event.

### Gaps
- No official X documentation on link ranking was retrieved. Buffer's full methodology could not be read (fetch blocked).
- No authoritative 2025–2026 data on LinkedIn or Threads link penalties was found in this pass.
- Platform-specific length or tone limits (for example LinkedIn's 3,000-character cap, Threads' 500 characters) were not verified with sources in this pass.

## 3. Which channel drives newsletter subscriptions (evidence quality)

### Takeaway
The best-sourced figure is **platform-internal**: Substack's network (recommendations plus app) reportedly drives about **50% of new free and 25% of new paid subscriptions** (early 2024, secondhand). One secondary source gives about 30% instead, so the two conflict. For X there is no clean subscriber-attribution data; the evidence is indirect (link posts suppressed in reach). Creator anecdotes vary widely. **Evidence quality: low to moderate overall.**

### Cited Findings
- Substack network → about 50% of new subscriptions, 25% of new paid (early 2024, attributed to Substack, secondhand). — [writestack.io: Substack recommendations](https://www.writestack.io/blog/substack-recommendations); [notetrail](https://notetrail.substack.com/p/how-to-grow-your-substack-in-2026). **Conflict:** another post credits Substack data with about 30% from in-platform discovery. — [wanderwealth](https://wanderwealth.substack.com/p/whats-the-1-way-to-grow-on-substack)
- Single-writer anecdotes: Notes produced 62% of the last 500 subscribers ([thrivewithcarrie](https://thrivewithcarrie.substack.com/p/substack-notes-strategy-2026)); another writer had Notes at 25% and recommendations at more than 40% in 30 days ([landonpoburan](https://landonpoburan.substack.com/p/if-notes-disappeared-tomorrow-heres-how-to-grow-substack)); 11 Notes brought 200+ subscribers ([escapethecubicle](https://escapethecubicle.substack.com/p/these-11-substack-notes-brought-me)). Self-reported and not representative.
- X: non-Premium link posts at median 0 engagement since March 2025 (Buffer, n=18.8M posts). This is indirect evidence that X click-through to newsletters is weak without Premium or a reply-link pattern. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/links-on-x/) (snippet)
- beehiiv: Boosts reportedly drive 10–20% net-new growth for users; beehiiv grew to about 140K newsletters in 2025 (stats roundup, secondary). — [shno.co](https://www.shno.co/marketing-statistics/newsletter-statistics)
- beehiiv reports split acquisition sources into website, recommendation, referral and embed; social platforms are not broken out natively. — [beehiiv support: Subscribers report](https://www.beehiiv.com/support/article/14492955466007-understanding-your-subscribers-report)
- Paid social cost per subscriber is $1–3 "when done well" (operator commentary on beehiiv's 2024 report). — [Newsletter Operator](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/takeaways-predictions-beehiivs-2024-state-newsletters-report)
- SparkLoop vendor claim: subscribers it acquires are 2x more likely to meet engagement criteria than beehiiv Boosts (vendor, biased). SparkLoop was acquired by ConvertKit/Kit. — [sparkloop.app](https://sparkloop.app/grow-on-beehiiv); [create.beehiiv.com](https://create.beehiiv.com/p/newsletter-exits)
- Lenny and Bloom: X was historically "really important" for early growth (2019–2022 era, before link suppression). — [Lenny](https://lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-i-built-a-1m-subscriber-newsletter)

### Inferences
- Since 2024–2025, for a Substack writer, **Substack's internal network (Notes, recommendations, app) is likely the highest-yield channel**. X and LinkedIn act more as top-of-funnel brand building with lower direct conversion. Medium acts as an SEO/discovery surface that funnels to Substack via in-story CTAs. This is directional and rests on weak evidence.

### Gaps
- No beehiiv or SparkLoop report breaking out subscriber acquisition by X vs LinkedIn vs Medium was found.
- No Press Gazette or Nieman Lab data on X referral decline to newsletters was retrieved in this pass.
- No quantitative Medium→Substack conversion-rate data was found.

## 4. Tooling and API constraints

### Takeaway
**Substack has no official posting API.** Automation relies on RSS (read-only triggers) or unofficial, cookie-based private endpoints, which may violate the Terms of Service. **Medium stopped issuing new API integration tokens on January 1, 2025.** Make deprecated its Medium app, and new programmatic Medium publishing is effectively closed, leaving Medium's Import-from-URL tool as the human-in-the-loop path. **X API is pay-per-use** (2026; legacy Basic was migrated after June 1, 2026), at about $0.015 per post and **about $0.20 per post containing a URL**, per third-party summaries. Schedulers (Typefully, Hypefury, Buffer, Publer) cover X, LinkedIn, Threads and Bluesky, and Typefully also covers Substack Notes. None publishes Substack long-form posts.

### Cited Findings
**Substack**
- No public REST API. Integrations are limited to RSS feeds, email-based posting and unofficial endpoints. — [api-evangelist/substack (GitHub)](https://github.com/api-evangelist/substack)
- Substack MCP server: drives the private, undocumented API using the `substack.sid` browser cookie, throttles to about 1 request/second, and warns that publishing is public and irreversible. — [codalanguez/substack-mcp](https://github.com/codalanguez/substack-mcp)
- n8n: copy a browser network request (endpoint, payload, `connect.sid` cookie) into an HTTP node. The author advises storing the cookie securely and using a least-privilege account, and notes it is "not official". — [Paweł Huryn Substack note](https://substack.com/@huryn/note/c-181571328). A community n8n node wraps the `substack-api` library (Notes and Posts); its docs describe API-key auth, which is inconsistent with the cookie approach, so verify before use. — [n8n-nodes-substack docs](https://n8n-nodes-substack.readthedocs.io/en/latest/introduction/)
- An unofficial posting API is reviewed as a ToS "gray area"; Substack could block it or flag accounts (promotional source). — [aiindigo review](https://aiindigo.com/blog/review-unofficial-substack-posting-api-2026-edition)
- RSS → Zapier → social: an auto-post of new Substack articles to X/Facebook. — [jmcglock Substack](https://jmcglock.substack.com/p/automatically-post-substack-articles)

**Medium**
- New integration tokens stopped on January 1, 2025; existing tokens reportedly continue to work. — [StackOne docs](https://docs.stackone.com/connectors/medium/guides/link-account/integration-token.md)
- Make: the Medium app is deprecated, and new tokens cannot be generated with Medium modules. — [apps.make.com/medium](https://apps.make.com/medium); [Make community](https://community.make.com/t/integrating-medium-with-make-com/65838)
- Write.as ended its Medium integration (a developer says access was cut without warning). — [write.as blog](https://write.as/blog/page/3)
- Medium RSS feeds remain readable; native scheduling exists. — [timetopost.co](https://timetopost.co/blog/medium-api-auto-post-2026/)
- Workaround repos exist (browser automation, "publishing without API"); ToS risk. — [medium-publishing-without-api](https://gh.r-mj.com/iancarson/medium-publishing-without-api)

**X API**
- No Basic tier for new developers. Legacy Basic ($200/month) moved to pay-per-use after June 1, 2026. Choices are pay-per-use or Enterprise. Posting costs about $0.015 per post and $0.20 per post with a URL (one source says the URL rate rose from $0.01 in April 2026). Reads cost about $0.005 per post, with a cap of 2M or 3M per month (disputed). All third-party; X's official pricing page was not fetched. — [Blotato](https://www.blotato.com/blog/twitter-api-pricing); [Outstand](https://www.outstand.so/blog/x-api-pricing); [Postproxy](https://postproxy.dev/blog/x-api-pricing-2026/); [PostEverywhere](https://posteverywhere.ai/blog/x-twitter-api-pricing); [upload-post](https://www.upload-post.com/x-api-pricing/)

**Schedulers**
- Typefully: publishes to X (posts, threads, X Articles), LinkedIn, Threads, Bluesky, Mastodon and **Substack Notes**. Per-platform toggles and per-platform edits are supported. LinkedIn cross-posting requires the Creator plan (about $16/month; Starter about $9, third-party pricing). It has a "Thread Finisher" closing-post feature. No evidence of an auto-plug-to-newsletter feature. — [Typefully support: what and where can I publish](https://support.typefully.com/en/articles/8728077-what-and-where-can-i-publish-with-typefully); [Typefully support: cross-post to LinkedIn](https://support.typefully.com/en/articles/8718168-publish-cross-post-to-linkedin); [kleo.so review](https://kleo.so/blog/typefully-review) (snippet only; fetch blocked)
- Hypefury: **Auto-plug** (replies with a promo/newsletter CTA once a post passes an engagement threshold), **auto-retweet** of top posts after a 4–12h delay, and **evergreen recycling** into empty slots. From about $29/month (reviews). Hypefury's own documentation warns that aggressive auto-DMs can trigger X spam filters. — [hypefury.com/llm-info](https://hypefury.com/llm-info); [makerstack review](https://makerstack.co/reviews/hypefury-review/); [Tweet Hunter review](https://tweethunter.io/blog/hypefury-review)
- Buttondown changelog shows a newsletter → X, LinkedIn, Bluesky and Threads posting routine. — [buttondown changelog 2024-06-19](https://buttondown.com/changelog/2024-06-19)
- Buffer, Publer: not researched in detail this pass (buffer.com blocked).

### Inferences
- Suggested agent architecture: **Substack = draft generation plus a human-paste or unofficial-API step behind explicit user opt-in (ToS risk flagged)**. **Medium = Import-from-URL by the human after Substack publishes** (keeps the canonical), or a legacy token if the user has one. **X / LinkedIn / Threads / Notes = through a scheduler API (Typefully or Buffer) or X pay-per-use directly**. The cost model should account for about 13x higher X API cost for URL posts, which again favours link-in-reply (whether a reply with a URL is also charged $0.20 is unknown). **RSS of the Substack feed = trigger** for downstream atomization.

### Gaps
- Official X developer pricing page not verified. Whether replies with URLs incur the URL rate is unverified.
- Typefully, Buffer and Publer public API capabilities (for example a Typefully API for programmatic drafts) were not verified in this pass.
- Zapier official Substack/Medium integration pages were not fetched (Zapier has historically offered only RSS triggers for Substack; unverified for 2026).
- LinkedIn and Threads API posting rules and limits were not researched here.

## 5. Measurement and attribution (UTM, cross-platform funnels)

### Takeaway
Substack offers built-in **traffic-source and subscriber-source rankings** per post and in Audience → Growth. It accepts UTM-tagged inbound links, but granular on-page attribution (which link inside a post was clicked) is not available. Traffic leaders often differ from subscriber leaders. Best practice is consistent UTM `source/medium/campaign` per platform and per piece, plus optional GA4 on custom domains.

### Cited Findings
- Substack post stats include "Traffic sources" (ranked views by source) and "Subscriber sources" (ranked new free and paid subscribers by source). — [Substack: post stats in the app](https://on.substack.com/p/post-stats-substack-app); [Substack: new growth chart and tools](https://on.substack.com/p/a-new-growth-chart-and-tools-to-help)
- Audience → Growth dashboard shows where viewers and free and paid subscribers come from. — [pubstacksuccess: Substack stats](https://pubstacksuccess.substack.com/p/substack-stats-how-to); [timomason analytics guide](https://timomason.substack.com/p/the-ultimate-substack-analytics-guide)
- Traffic ≠ subscriber attribution; the largest traffic source may not be the largest subscriber source. Timing-based attribution is correlation; only UTM links give direct click tracking (vendor). — [Narrareach: subscriber attribution](https://www.narrareach.com/features/subscriber-attribution-substack); [Narrareach: traffic sources explained](https://www.narrareach.com/blog/substack-traffic-sources-explained)
- Limitation: there is no way to see where inside a newsletter a link was clicked (UTM medium cannot be used for intra-page placement). — [simonkjones Substack](https://simonkjones.substack.com/p/understanding-substacks-analytics)
- UTM convention example: `utm_source=substack&utm_medium=<publication>&utm_campaign=guestpost`; review in GA4. — [reallygoodbusinessideas: GA for Substack](https://www.reallygoodbusinessideas.com/p/google-analytics-substack)
- A subscriber export attribution field includes values like `substack-notes`, `substack-signup-flow` and `direct`, which can be classified with SQL. The schema may change. — [stackcontacts Substack](https://stackcontacts.substack.com/p/stop-flying-blind-get-data-driven)
- beehiiv: New Subscribers by acquisition source (website, recommendation, referral, embed). — [beehiiv support](https://www.beehiiv.com/support/article/14492955466007-understanding-your-subscribers-report)

### Inferences
- Agent module: generate a UTM per (platform × piece × placement), for example `utm_source=x&utm_medium=reply_plug&utm_campaign=<post-slug>`. Log publish timestamps per platform. Pull Substack subscriber-source CSV exports for joining. Report a "traffic vs subscribers vs paid" funnel per channel. Note that X/LinkedIn in-app browsers and link shorteners may strip referrers, so UTMs carry the attribution.

### Gaps
- Whether Substack's subscriber-source report surfaces UTM values directly (vs only referrer domains) was not confirmed from Substack docs.
- No cross-platform multi-touch attribution study for creators was found.
