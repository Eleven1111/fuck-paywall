## Per-creator core beliefs, routines, frameworks, editing tests and advice (with quotes)

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced material (full transcripts) is for **Sahil Bloom** (Dec 2023), **Alex Hormozi** (2023–2026), the **Cultural Tutor** (Aug 2023), **Harry Dry** (Jul 2024), **Jack Butcher** (Aug 2021) and **Sam Parr** (Oct 2023). Their methods are concrete and quotable: Bloom's "writing from conversation" and Twitter-as-compression tool; Hormozi's "Rule of One", "Proof → Promise → Plan" and "vague → clear" packaging; Dry's three sentence tests; the Cultural Tutor's "what's going to make me roar" daily thread. Two of the framework names in the brief are verified: Ship 30's **4A** (from the earlier note) and Welsh's **content matrix**, which Welsh says he has since **stopped using**. I could not verify Cole's "category creation" as a writing framework. "Category of One" is the subtitle of Cole's Category Pirates book *Snow Leopard*.

### Cited Findings

#### Sahil Bloom — How I Write with David Perell, 2023-12-20 [T] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f-s22uCixMw) (transcript in the [GitHub repo](https://github.com/liamdonnellyonline-cell/david-perell-nonfiction-transcripts))
- **Writing from conversation (pre-test ideas by talking):** "bringing the essence of an idea very quickly and concisely to somebody try to distill it to someone see where you didn't make sense or where they were like looking confused yeah I call this writing from conversation you're talking to people and you're getting a sense for what's Landing what's not Landing."
- **Twitter as a compression tool when stuck:** "if I'm working on a piece and I'm stuck on something I'll have this feeling that whatever it is that I'm writing needs to get shorter more distilled and I will literally pop open Twitter and I will write that out and try to meet the character constraint and just shorter and shorter and shorter."
- **Constraint and cutting:** "forced constraints are a really good way to stoke creativity … forcing yourself to try to write it into shorter bite-sized things to cut more and more and more … when in doubt just cut." He uses *Green Eggs and Ham* (49 distinct words) as the example.
- **Pushing through the "creative cliff illusion":** he cites a study finding that people wrongly expect their best ideas to come early in a session. In fact "it's through that kind of like trudging through the mud of the creative session that you end up unlocking your most creative ideas." Perell adds Ed Sheeran's "dirty tap water" analogy.
- **How he finds the angle (known idea + personal struggle):** "take something that is known an idea that's interesting … and then what is my way of wrestling with it like how has it applied to my life in a unique or interesting way how have I struggled with that problem … and how can I help people … struggle with it a little bit better." Packaging over novelty: "I would guess 95% of those [power-law successes] are just an idea or set of ideas that already existed that are put into a slightly more beautiful package." He gives James Clear's *Atomic Habits* as the example.
- **Quality judgment: utility over eloquence.** "the sentences that I've written that seem to really impact people are not the ones that I thought were the most eloquent … often they're just really mundane things … there isn't a huge correlation sometimes between how hard you work on something and how well it ends up doing." His word-level test: "are you writing that word because you want other people to be like wow … you're so smart or are you writing that word because it adds incremental utility to the sentence." He says he does not want to "impress the smartest people in the room."
- **Origin of his thread format:** his first thread was in 2020 at about 500 followers, before X had a native thread feature. He modeled it on Ben Thompson's and David Senra's habit of cross-linking past ideas: "build on ideas that I had written about previously by casing them back into a new idea … basically create a Twitter native blog." On the 2021 thread boom: "everyone and their mother is writing threads most of them unfortunately um are bad."
- **Idea incubation ritual:** he deliberately sits with an idea instead of moving on: "if you spark that idea in your gut … and then you go off and do a million other things … the idea never really gets to take hold." He calls the middle layer of his creativity system (thinking, bouncing ideas off people, the Feynman technique) "that beautiful sludgy yucky middle layer of creativity that's the real key."
- **Against vanity metrics:** "focus on vanity metrics dilutes the quality … much much better off having 10,000 people that are truly connected with you … than by reaching a 100,000 people by posting about TED Talks." He cites Kevin Kelly's *1,000 True Fans*. He also refuses lead-gen tactics he finds cringe even when they would add subscribers.
- **Mission framing:** "every piece of writing is a call for the kind of person that you want to bring into your life."
- **Newsletter growth (not craft):** the first inflection came in May 2021, when he started sending on a real cadence. The second, around Jan 2023, came from hiring a growth lead (lead magnets, SparkLoop, paid ads at break-even), taking growth from "5 to 10,000 a month to 50,000 a month."

#### Alex Hormozi — YouTube talks [T] (the repo has no dates; upload dates are not verified unless noted)
- **Rule of One (borrowed from Michael Masterson's *Great Leads*):** Masterson "reviewed the top 100 offers of all time … 91 of them all centered around a single concept … the one big idea … has to be easy to understand, easy to believe, and interesting or unique." "Toss salad copy" means stacking many benefits. All sub-bullets "should only be bullets that reinforce the singular message." "The hard part is figuring out what that one idea is going to be." He keeps the rule "right on front of my computer." — [The Copy Rule I Learned From The Best Copywriter I Know](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OyGKFhTf0go)
- **Proof → Promise → Plan intro formula:** "Proof promise plan … proof is that you prove that you know what you're talking about. You give people a reason to believe you. The promise is to tell them what they're going to get … And the plan sets the expectations of what will happen next." The order is flexible ("If you have a promise that's incredibly important … lead with that"). He leads with proof because business viewers have "this big glaring sign above their head … which is why should I listen to you?" — [If I Wanted To Grow An Audience In 2026 I'd Do This](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Jmkq5RLjm0U)
- **Six shifts after 40 months (78M followers):** "number one going from edutainment to education number two for us to for you number three wide to narrow number four views to revenue number five shorts to Longs number six assume more to assume nothing."
  - **Packaging should be "vague to clear."** He pushes back on the "pervasive narrative … you got to have curiosity" (the curiosity gap).
  - **Use data visuals** instead of flashy B-roll.
  - **"Pre-search" instead of post-production.**
  - **Measure ad revenue, not views**, as the leading metric.
  - **"Assume nothing"**: don't write as if the viewer already knows you.

  — [How I Gained 78 Million Followers In 40 Months: 6 Key Lessons](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HVOubeXUcx0)
- **Proof is the moat against AI:** "AI will not equally disrupt all creators … as you move along this continuum, when the stakes get higher, you're less and less likely to listen to someone who does not have third-party proof." "How can I provide proof at scale in real time?" A tactic: transcribe your real meetings and ask AI for "the interesting moments … So, rather than you making up stories, you actually give real examples." He says his "whole content strategy is live and interactive." — [The New Way of Making Content In The Age of AI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XsWSvz-aewA)
- **Advice to beginners: years, not 90 days.** "people are making podcasts and thinking in 90 days they're somehow going to blow up … you're finding your voice for the first year." His first 150 podcast episodes were "just me talking about how to run a gym better." "Make the action the goal … did I make the post did I make the reach outs every day then you're a success." He also says "do more cool and then you'll have cool [stuff] to talk about rather than regur[gitating] other people's stuff." — [How to Grow an Audience if You Have 0 Followers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7ITff1fIbSc)
- **Test titles with no ego:** for *$100M Leads* he A/B tested the title word ("leads" beat promotion, advertising and marketing) and the subtitle. "How to get strangers to want to buy your stuff" beat the version without "How to" by "71% to 29% … just a one word … I just come in with like no ego about what I think it's going to be." He cites Tim Ferriss testing *The 4-Hour Workweek* title. — Perell interview, 2025-03-14 [T] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yCcySsSkmjs)

#### The Cultural Tutor (Alexander Learmonth), a top X thread writer — Perell interview, 2023-08-23 [T] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1pBxikHRCXI)
- **Write on the day the idea strikes:** "The best time to write about an idea is when it first strikes you. I want to find something that grips me that day because then the stakes are high … it's now or never. What's going to make me roar? That's what I want to get to."
- **Anti-batching and anti-scheduling** (the opposite of Welsh's method): asked whether he pre-writes and schedules: "No, no, no. I … write it every day. I just open Twitter … twitter.com, press upload tweet and start writing." On his writing routine: "I don't have a routine. I just write." He treats the daily thread as his single objective.
- **Standard for a good day:** "my aim is to write one good sentence every day. If I write one good sentence, it's been a good day."
- **On blocks and productivity hacks:** "no amount of productivity hacks will get you closer … either you do it or you don't." "to say I don't have the time to write means you don't want to write."
- **Perell's interjection (block = wrong topic):** "the biggest cause of writer's block isn't actually writer's block. It's just that you're not writing what you're supposed to be writing." The cure is to bring the topic into your own personal experience so it "can sort of roar."

#### Harry Dry (Marketing Examples; a big X copywriting account) — Perell, 2024-07-17 [T] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TUMjnmfsPeM)
- **Three sentence-level tests:** "Can I visualize it? Can I falsify it? Can nobody else say this? So, you get three nos. You've probably written a lot of rubbish. You get three yeses. You're on to something."
  - **Visualize (concrete beats abstract):** "seamless transition, charging pitbull, muesli Irishman, better way, leg of lamb" — people remember the ones they can picture.
  - **Falsify:** a falsifiable sentence means "you're putting your head on the chopping block." His heuristic is "you can't talk. You can only point."
  - **Nobody else:** "never write an ad a competitor can sign" (attributed to Jim Durfee). Positive example: New Balance, "worn by supermodels in London and dads in Ohio." Negative example: "change an entire industry," which fails all three tests.
- **Idea generation by collision:** in the demo, he built a piece by combining a Visualize Value tweet ("1% to 2%" conversions) with a Volkswagen speedometer ad. He looks for a "conflict" (opposing pairs) to drive the piece.

#### Jack Butcher (Visualize Value) — "Get Going then Get Good" workshop with Perell, 2021-08-13 [T, outside the 2022+ window] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EGnSx3EYEU0)
- **Sawdust and proof of work:** "there is … an incredible amount of material that is produced from the act of producing something … it's a simple tweak in how you think but it can really arm you with a lot of … assets that you can publish."
- **Iterations over hours:** "I love this idea from naval it's not 10 000 hours that creates outliers it's 10 000 iterations." On repetition without learning: "the majority of people don't have 20 years experience they have one year of experience repeated 20 times."
- **Years of zero reception:** his 2010 blog had no readers, and early Visualize Value posts on Facebook got "literally zero reception … friends and family are like oh jack's doing this cute graphic design thing." It only spread once he moved to Twitter, an open network.
- **Niche from what's counterintuitive:** he found his focus by spotting what he was "uniquely good at which was counterintuitive in the world of marketing and advertising." The resulting offer: "if you have something complicated to explain i can help you visualize it."

#### Sam Parr (MFM co-host, The Hustle) — Perell, 2023-10-11 [T] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CO2ELWa3jsc)
- **Remix and word economy:** "usually what I do is I steal from a lot of stuff and I mash it together … every word has to serve a purpose." His models are 1970s–80s WSJ and NYT ads. He passes on Harry Dry's line: "copy is like food how it looks is as important as how it tastes."
- **Simplicity:** Hemingway and other great authors "write like a fifth grade reading level … you can like create something that … has a lot of meaning to it with simple language."
- **Don't flex credentials:** Perell's point in the conversation: "they'll try to flex … I'm the chief marketing officer … don't write any of that."
- **Thinking is the bar:** "you don't [have to] think clearly necessarily to be a good speaker but you have to think clearly in order to be a great writer."

#### Marc Andreessen (inventor of the tweetstorm) — Perell, 2023-10-04 [T] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tV6kDr1rwTs)
- **Origin of the tweetstorm:** "the tweet storm is just that I have too much to say and you know, this 140 character thing is just like obviously inadequate … I'll just keep adding on tweets." He faced "blow back … you're abusing the medium."
- **Recurring formats:** "There are certain like just atomic units of media that just seem to recur over and over in history." He says Nietzsche would be "the best tweeter of all time."
- **Barbell reading diet:** "either stuff that is super current, uh, or it's stuff that's timeless … trying to not read anything that's from yesterday through to like 10 years ago." He agrees he writes best with "a lot of energy," or "a little bit of a rage."
- He now rarely tweets for reputational-risk reasons and prefers essays: "the essay really gives you a chance to like fully articulate yourself."

#### Naval Ravikant, via his compiler Eric Jorgenson — Perell, 2020-09-16 [T, pre-2022] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mWYMRCcYn-Q)
- **Compression trade-off:** "there's a tough trade-off you make as you keep distilling where you start to lose people the more precise you get … if you distill all the way down to productize yourself … nobody knows what you're talking about." Readers who can't "unfold the word compounding into two pages" are lost. "I don't think naval has the patience to go like write out the two pages of compounding." Jorgenson's book is the "unfold" layer between the tweetstorm and full explanations. The aphorism is the compressed form, and the long-form podcast or book is the decompression.
- I found **no** first-person Naval interview describing *how* he drafts tweets [SN]. A Substack recap says he followed the 2018 tweetstorm with podcasts explaining each tweet. — [wisdomproject.substack](https://wisdomproject.substack.com/p/the-wisdom-of-naval-ravikant?open=false)

### Inferences
- The strongest first-person evidence comes from people who explain craft through **tests applied to sentences**: Dry's 3 tests, Bloom's "utility vs. flex" word test, Hormozi's Rule of One, Bush's "clear not clever." These are more reliable for building on than growth-hack folklore.
- **Compression and decompression are one system:** Naval's tweets are unfolded by Jorgenson and the podcasts; Bloom compresses in the tweet box when stuck; Bush says every essay starts as a tweet. Tweets serve as the thesis test, and long-form is where the idea is unfolded.

## Cross-creator principles, disagreements, and evidence-based vs. folklore

### Takeaway
There is strong agreement on: clarity over cleverness, one idea per piece, writing in spoken plain language, compressing as a thinking tool, finding the angle in personal experience or proof, and a time horizon of years. The real disagreements are **batch/schedule vs. write-the-day's-obsession**, **curiosity-gap hooks vs. "vague → clear" packaging**, and **novelty vs. repackaging**. Only a few claims rest on anything like data: Masterson's 91/100 offers (anecdotal, via Hormozi), Hormozi's title A/B tests and channel metrics (self-reported), and the "creative cliff illusion" study Bloom cites (published research, not checked here).

  - These can be reconciled: the *idea* may be old, but the *evidence, experience and framing* must be yours.
- **Disagreement: is effort correlated with results?**
  - Bloom: "there isn't a huge correlation … between how hard you work on something and how well it ends up doing" — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f-s22uCixMw)
  - Ship 30 and Welsh systems assume volume plus iteration improves the hit rate (prior note).
  - Not really opposed: Bloom's point argues for more shots on goal rather than polishing each one.
- **Evidence status:**
  - (a) The Masterson 91/100 is secondhand via Hormozi and not independently checked — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OyGKFhTf0go)
  - (b) Hormozi's 71/29 subtitle test is self-reported with no sample size — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yCcySsSkmjs)
  - (c) Hormozi's "views → revenue" and "shorts → longs" claims rest on his own channel data, not public datasets — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HVOubeXUcx0)
  - (d) Bloom's "creative cliff illusion" refers to a real study; I did not retrieve the paper — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f-s22uCixMw)
  - Everything else is practitioner experience with survivorship bias.

### Inferences
- **Most evidence-backed** (supported by copy testing or cognitive science, not just X folklore): concreteness and imageability (Dry's "visualize," consistent with memory research on concrete words), single-message focus, and A/B testing headlines.
- **Folklore-leaning:** exact posting cadences, the claim that same-day writing beats batching, and claims that thread lengths or curiosity gaps reliably win.
- The batching disagreement probably follows writer type. **Systems writers** (Welsh, Koe, Ship 30) batch, and **obsession writers** (Cultural Tutor, Andreessen writing "in a rage," Naval) write when gripped. An agent should ask which mode a user is in.

### Gaps
- No controlled data compares batching with same-day posting, or curiosity hooks with clear hooks, on X specifically.
- The "creative cliff illusion" paper (Lucas & Nordgren, 2015, as I recall it) was not verified in this session.

## What can become agent checklists/prompts vs. what must stay human

### Takeaway
Sentence-level tests, structural formulas and packaging tests turn cleanly into agent checklists: Dry ×3, Rule of One, Proof-Promise-Plan, intention/obstacle/stakes, clear-not-clever, utility-vs-flex, compress-to-280, read-in-a-new-medium. What has to stay human: choosing the idea that "makes me roar," real proof and lived experience (Hormozi insists that inventing stories fails), conversation-testing with real people, and the long-horizon commitment.

### Cited Findings
- **Automatable sentence tests:**
  - Dry's "Can I visualize it? Can I falsify it? Can nobody else say this?" — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TUMjnmfsPeM)
  - Bloom's word test ("adds incremental utility" vs. "sound smart") — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f-s22uCixMw)
- **Automatable structure:**
  - Rule of One (one big idea that is easy to understand, easy to believe and unique; every sub-bullet reinforces it) — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OyGKFhTf0go)
  - Proof → Promise → Plan opener — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Jmkq5RLjm0U)
  - Puri's intention + obstacle + stakes — [Recapio](https://recapio.com/digest/masterclass-in-storytelling-for-beginners-with-shaan-puri-by-david-perell)
- **Automatable compression drill:** rewrite the thesis under 280 characters, "shorter and shorter" (Bloom) — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f-s22uCixMw). Bush: "every essay I write starts as a tweet" — [quotewise](https://quotewise.io/q/w8hnsw/). The reverse "unfold" step (Jorgenson): expand each compressed term for readers who lack the background — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mWYMRCcYn-Q)
- **Automatable packaging:** generate many title variants and test them without ego (Hormozi's 71/29) — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yCcySsSkmjs). Check "assume nothing" (does it make sense to a stranger?) and "vague → clear" — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HVOubeXUcx0)
- **AI-assisted but human-sourced proof:** transcribe real meetings and ask AI for "the interesting moments," instead of "making up stories" — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XsWSvz-aewA)
- **Must stay human:**
  - The obsession filter: "What's going to make me roar?" (Cultural Tutor) — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1pBxikHRCXI)
  - "Ideas have people" and the mission; real conversation testing (Bloom) — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f-s22uCixMw)
  - Third-party proof, which is the high-stakes trust signal AI can't fake (Hormozi) — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XsWSvz-aewA)
  - Sawdust from real work (Butcher) — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EGnSx3EYEU0)

### Inferences
Draft agent checklist, synthesized from the cited sources above:
1. **Angle:** state the one big idea in one sentence. Is it easy to understand, easy to believe and unique? Which personal struggle or proof makes it yours?
2. **Opener:** proof, promise, plan (in any order). For stories, intention + obstacle + stakes.
3. **Each sentence:** visualize, falsify, nobody else; utility, not flex; clear, not clever.
4. **Compression:** a ≤280-character thesis; cut anything you doubt.
5. **Stranger test:** assume nothing; define any jargon an outsider would trip on.
6. **Read-aloud and change-of-medium pass:** voice memo (Puri) and Kindle (Mack, [W]).
7. **Packaging:** generate 10+ title variants for a human or data test.
8. **Human gate:** does the author feel gripped? Is the proof real? Has it been said out loud to someone?

An agent should never invent anecdotes or metrics. The creators treat proof as the moat.

### Gaps
- No creator interview found describes their own use of AI agents for writing, apart from Koe (prior note) and Hormozi's meeting-transcript tactic.
- I could not check whether Welsh's later, post-matrix process replaced idea generation with something else that could be automated.
