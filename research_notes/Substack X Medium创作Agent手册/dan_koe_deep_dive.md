## 1. Ideation & idea capture: how he finds ideas, and which framework names hold up

### Takeaway
Koe's ideation runs on two inputs: (1) "validated" ideas he finds by studying outlier and top-performing content, often his own old tweets or other creators' best videos, and (2) ideas that come up while walking, reading and talking, because the week's newsletter is his "top of mind goal." He openly rejects elaborate second-brain systems ("embrace the mess"). Several framework names in the brief could not be verified: "idea bank," "content multiplication" and "APAG" did not show up in his own words in what I could access. The terms he does use are "validated ideas/topics," "outlier content," "swipe file," "reverse engineer," "packaging" and "one topic or theme for the week."

### Cited Findings
- **Against second brains:** "I actually think building a second brain or a system for your ideas is like a false god. Because systems succumb to entropy… I don't care how perfect your notion system is… it's going to fall apart one day… Embrace the mess. Embrace the chaos." [transcript, KCjwU4XYBL8 @18:31–19:03] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KCjwU4XYBL8) / [mirror](https://github.com/tjxj/dankoe-archive)
- **On lost ideas:** "If you need to remember it, then it's not important. And if it's important, you'll remember it. The act of writing it down is just the act of putting your thoughts into words." [transcript, KCjwU4XYBL8 @19:25] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KCjwU4XYBL8)
- **His list of what the system needs:** "You need one topic or theme for the week… for me, that's my newsletter. You need a place to quickly jot down ideas. You need a way to capture inspiration. So social posts, YouTube videos, Substack articles… PDFs, images. You need to understand how to package the hook or title… writing or content is like a gift. You have the actual contents… Then you have the packaging… And lastly, you need to effectively pull from your ideas, inspiration, and research while you're actually writing." [transcript, KCjwU4XYBL8 @19:25–20:33] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KCjwU4XYBL8)
- **The weekly goal drives passive capture:** "I have a newsletter that I write every single week that turns into this YouTube video and turns into other content, but that is my sole focus… when I go on walks or I listen to conversations, ideas just pop up out of nowhere and somehow connect themselves to my newsletter… I'm collecting ideas just by living my life." He credits this mechanism to Psycho-Cybernetics. [transcript, KCjwU4XYBL8 @13:15–14:14] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KCjwU4XYBL8)
- **His two principles for picking a topic:** (1) "is it something that I find important or worth sharing? Because by simply filtering an idea through my perspective… it becomes unique"; (2) "is it packaged or framed in a way that grabs attention… There's journaling and then there's writing." [transcript, KCjwU4XYBL8 @21:16–21:51] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KCjwU4XYBL8)
- **Market research before writing:** "Before you post the YouTube video, look at titles that work… you need to read good tweets… high performing tweets over and over. You need to read more Substack articles that have done well and ask yourself, why did this do so well compared to mine… You do what works, but with your own spin." [transcript, KCjwU4XYBL8 @22:47–24:03] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KCjwU4XYBL8)
- **Concrete example of a borrowed title:** The July 2026 video's topic came from "a video that got like 700,000 views… called the Notebook System that saved my brain. I looked at that and I'm like, What about the writing system that saved my brain?" [transcript, KCjwU4XYBL8 @25:48] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KCjwU4XYBL8)
- **Tactic: sort a creator by top content:** "Look for a specific creator and then go to their actual account and then filter their content by top… then if you want to learn how to actually recreate it, just hit the reverse engineer button." The "reverse engineer button" is a feature of his app Eden. He also names "one of ten" (1of10, a YouTube outlier tool) as an alternative. [transcript, KCjwU4XYBL8 @24:39–25:48] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KCjwU4XYBL8)
- **"Validated ideas," his own example:** He wrote a tweet in about 2019 that began "how to get ahead of 99% of people." In 2023 he turned it into a YouTube video, "now my highest performing YouTube video at 1.7 million views." He says Mark Manson then used the same title structure (about 4.5M views), followed by Hormozi and Tom Bilyeu. [transcript, 7HM-rptYdTs @14:49–15:40] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7HM-rptYdTs)
- **Topics wear out:** "Some topics that used to do well are kind of just dead now, or they've been exhausted to the point where they need a reset period." [transcript, 7HM-rptYdTs @15:40] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7HM-rptYdTs)
- **Where he draws the copying line:** "Nobody has a novel idea… The way that they worded it, the way that they structured it can be considered theirs… you're going to look like an idiot if you just… copy paste it, change a few words… but you can take the idea." [transcript, 7HM-rptYdTs @11:55–12:52] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7HM-rptYdTs)
- **Re-mining his own back catalog:** He searches his own top-performing posts "so I can iterate off of those. And I do that all the time. That's really how I continue growing at this point." [transcript, 7HM-rptYdTs @19:19–19:51] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7HM-rptYdTs)
- **Learning by repetition:** When he falls into a topic (e.g., Claude Code/AI, nutrition), "I'm just going to watch the same topic over and over again so that I get all of the nuances." - **Ad hoc capture isn't enough:** "Another thing people do is they just jot down ideas as they come to mind, and hope that those ideas are good enough… they're not really strategized… they're just time consuming and they're slow." He says this right before pitching Eden's outlier discover feed. [transcript, 7HM-rptYdTs @18:17] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7HM-rptYdTs)
- **Greg Isenberg interview (Oct 6, 2025), "Inside Dan Koe's AI Content Engine":** Episode chapters reportedly include "how Dan generates newsletter ideas" (~4:45), "using Twitter to validate content ideas" (~10:30), "tools for finding viral content" (~14:19) and "AI workflow and prompts" (~36:32). A Chinese recap says weekly newsletter topics usually come from a top-performing tweet or a popular YouTube video in his niche. [snippet] — [audioscrape episode page](https://audioscrape.com/podcast/the-startup-ideas-podcast/episode/inside-dan-koes-ai-content-engine-65200); [podwise](https://podwise.ai/episodes/5374763); [woshipm recap (ZH)](https://www.woshipm.com/ai/6283902.html); [YouTube HhspudqFSvU, as linked by fan repo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HhspudqFSvU)
- **Newsletter title (verified via the ZH mirror's link list):** "You don't need a niche, you need a point of view." [ZH summary] — [letters.thedankoe.com/p/you-dont-need-a-niche-you-need-a](https://letters.thedankoe.com/p/you-dont-need-a-niche-you-need-a). He repeats the theme on video: "you are the niche. You are the most valuable resource, your point of view." [transcript, 3rNqNvwNcrM @14:29] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3rNqNvwNcrM)

### Inferences
- His ideation is **market-first plus perspective-filter**: borrow a validated hook or title structure, then write his own argument under it. He is explicit that the content is his and the packaging is borrowed or studied.
- A "swipe file" is real in his practice: he describes "my YouTube swipe file" and "post swipe file" as Eden boards, and a "database of YouTube videos and titles." I found no verbatim use of the name "idea bank."

### Gaps
- **Unverified framework names:** "content multiplication," "idea bank," "APAG," and a named "hook / aha / practical steps" newsletter outline. None appeared in the transcripts or ZH summaries I could search. He says outlining and structuring newsletters is "the topic for an entirely new video" and points people to a community-made prompt based on his top Substack articles (KCjwU4XYBL8 @29:33). The Isenberg episode's exact prompts sit behind a "blueprint" download ([gregisenberg.com/content-engine](https://www.gregisenberg.com/content-engine), per the fan repo README) that I couldn't fetch.

## 2. Writing, outlining, editing & repurposing (X / Substack / YouTube / LinkedIn / IG / shorts)

### Takeaway
Koe runs one weekly "project": a single long newsletter. He publishes it to Substack and pastes it into X Articles. It becomes a loose YouTube script he riffs from an outline, and that video is reposted to podcast feeds. During the week the letter is mined for tweets. Those tweets are cross-posted to Substack Notes, turned into image posts for Instagram and LinkedIn, and some are read to camera as Reels, TikToks and Shorts. He deliberately posts *less* volume than the "post more" camp.

### Cited Findings
- **The full repurposing chain, in his words:** "I post one newsletter to Substack, which is also an article on Substack. I copy paste that to X articles… that eventually turns into a soft YouTube script, and then the YouTube video gets uploaded to podcast channels, and then I can use the original newsletter for tweet ideas throughout the week… And then those get cross posted to Substack notes, and then I turn them into images that I can post on Instagram and LinkedIn. And then for some of the tweets that I really like, I'll just sit in front of the camera and read the tweet to the camera and boom! Now I have reels, TikToks and shorts, and it only took an hour or two every day." [transcript, KCjwU4XYBL8 @26:06–26:45] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KCjwU4XYBL8)
- **Instagram format:** "all of my posts are just written posts in the form of an image." [transcript, KCjwU4XYBL8 @06:21] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KCjwU4XYBL8)
- **Going against volume (and Hormozi):** "Any more content than that, I know there's Alex Hormozi and other people who just want you to push more… volume. But everyone's doing that… Tone it down, increase the quality… Add a bit of scarcity to your brand so people are not just overloaded with it." [transcript, KCjwU4XYBL8 @26:45–27:09] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KCjwU4XYBL8)
- **Weekly project setup:** He makes a board (Eden; "of course, this can be a notion page") with an outline note of rough points, YouTube links pasted in as research, and an AI chat open alongside to "pull the ideas that I need" from those videos. He quick-captures new ideas into the board while writing. [transcript, KCjwU4XYBL8 @27:34–28:55] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KCjwU4XYBL8)
- **Packaging comes last:** "I can craft the title and thumbnail later on." For titles he references the newsletter in an AI chat and his saved title swipe file, or has it search a specific creator's titles "if I want them to be data backed." [transcript, KCjwU4XYBL8 @25:48 & @28:55] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KCjwU4XYBL8)
- **How the videos are made:** "I just had my newsletter open here. It's not like I read it word for word, but it's like an outline that I can riff off of." He says of another creator (Craig Perry) running "my playbook": "his YouTube bio is literally reading my newsletters to a camera." [transcript, 7HM-rptYdTs @09:39] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7HM-rptYdTs). On essay videos: "There's cuts that cut out everything but the outline of this video or the newsletter." [transcript, 3rNqNvwNcrM @21:59] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3rNqNvwNcrM)
- **Essay-writing guidance (Apr 2026):** "Write about what genuinely interests you. Focus on a single main idea. Use this as a time to research and learn. Go down rabbit holes… Do not accept one source as law. Resist the template… for now, just write. Have a debate with yourself. Ask questions to keep the writing going. Then worry about the structure… Do the thinking first. Ask, do I actually believe this?… the point of this is to change what you believe." [transcript, 3rNqNvwNcrM @23:40–24:04] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3rNqNvwNcrM)
- **"Fast content" vs "slow content":** He contrasts fast content (listicles, rage tweets, "AI generated summaries," "engagement optimized threads") with slow content (essays, long-form conversations, "even tweets crafted in a way that make you think to receive the insight"). [transcript, 3rNqNvwNcrM @11:15] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3rNqNvwNcrM)
- **Platform picks:** Start on Substack or X. "Substack, I think, is the most slept on social media… you own your audience… It's email first… they have a notes feed of people who actually appreciate deep and genuine thinking." He says he was "runner up in the $1 million challenge of the articles" on X. [transcript, 3rNqNvwNcrM @24:53–25:24] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3rNqNvwNcrM)
- **Write first, video later:** Writing first builds "a backlog of scripts." An audience on a writing platform then seeds YouTube: "your audience grows so much faster on YouTube. That's exactly what I did. And I'm just an introvert." [transcript, KCjwU4XYBL8 @05:44–06:21] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KCjwU4XYBL8)
- **Secondary framing:** Third-party write-ups describe the system as "one strong piece of content, then distribute it intelligently." [snippet] — [tagabukid Substack](https://tagabukid.substack.com/p/how-dan-koe-writes-one-newsletter). An editor who claims to have edited Koe's work wrote a post titled "My So-called Creator Life and Editing Dan Koe." I couldn't fetch it, so I don't know what it says about his editing process. — [otherwordy Substack](https://otherwordy.substack.com/p/my-so-called-creator-life-and-editing)

### Inferences
- In practice his "editing" means outline-first drafting, then packaging (hook, title, thumbnail) optimized against a swipe file, and on video, cutting down to the outline. I found no description in his own words of a separate line-editing pass.
- LinkedIn is a low-effort downstream channel for him: image reposts of tweets. It is not a native strategy.

### Gaps
- No primary detail on a newsletter section template, word count, or a dedicated editing or proofreading step. Whether he uses a human editor is unconfirmed; the otherwordy post title suggests at least one did editing work for him.

## 3. Daily/weekly workflow, time allocation & tools

### Takeaway
Koe consistently describes a 1–2 hour morning writing block. In his "$1M morning routine" letter this is a 30-minute walk plus 90 minutes of writing, followed by gym and only then email, meetings and non-creative work. For beginners he prescribes 60 minutes a day: 30 minutes to research validated posts and draft, 30 minutes on DM and reply networking. His tooling moved from Kortex (his note/second-brain app) to **Eden** (eden.so). Eden early access began Nov 24, 2025. It is a content-research and writing tool with boards, swipe files, an outlier "discover feed," creator top-content filters, a "reverse engineer" function and an AI chat.

### Cited Findings
- **The morning routine:** "Walk 30 minutes, write 90 minutes," then a small meal, another walk, a few messages, the gym, and only after that email, meetings and other non-creative work. ## 4. How he uses AI, and what he says not to use it for

### Takeaway
Koe uses AI heavily as an *iteration and research amplifier*. He uses it to generate variations of strong posts, query videos and PDFs in a board, brainstorm data-backed titles and reverse-engineer structures. He warns against handing it the thinking: "AI… can mimic what looks like real thinking," it "exhausts creativity extremely fast," and its default output "usually isn't that good." His position is that AI raises the bar, so taste and skill are the differentiator.

### Cited Findings
- **Variations, filtered through his own taste:** "One thing I like to actually use AI for is just that for iterations on those ideas… I take really good posts that I like and be like, hey, just give me iterations of these and I'll read through them and I'll just wait for one to hit… it goes through AI to give me an iteration, it goes through my mind because I'm usually not taking exactly what it says… By the end of that process, I usually have something that's pretty original." [transcript, 7HM-rptYdTs @13:51–14:49] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7HM-rptYdTs)
- **Breaking down another creator:** "Add a creator's top performing YouTube videos and just be like, hey, break all of these down. What is this person's worldview? How does this person structure videos? How can I apply this to the next video?" [transcript, 7HM-rptYdTs @19:51–20:18] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7HM-rptYdTs)
- **The double edge:** "The more I use AI, the more useful I find it… But on the other edge of the sword, I find that it exhausts creativity extremely fast because creativity is kind of sparse… it requires you to go out, do things, experience something novel." He also says, "You can tell AI to share something novel, but then you are anticipating it. It is no longer novel." [transcript, 3rNqNvwNcrM @15:49–16:07] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3rNqNvwNcrM)
- **AI can't hold your perspective:** "Only a human can write an essay because a robot doesn't have a situated point of view… It can simulate the perspective you tell it to adopt, but it lacks the beliefs, biases, and emotions… And honestly, I use AI a lot." [transcript, 3rNqNvwNcrM @13:58] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3rNqNvwNcrM)
- **AI speeds up imitation:** "AI accelerates content production, yes, but it also accelerates imitation… The issue is that it can mimic what looks like real thinking without requiring any cognitive effort from either the creator or the consumer." [transcript, 3rNqNvwNcrM @10:13] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3rNqNvwNcrM)
- **Superpowers for everyone:** "Yes, AI gives you superpowers, but when everyone has superpowers, nobody has superpowers." [transcript, 7HM-rptYdTs @06:07] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7HM-rptYdTs)
- **Don't accept the output as law:** AI is "a catalyst to avoid a lot of trial and error… [if you don't] know how to iterate or refine or not just accept the output of AI as law"; "the default response of the AI usually isn't that good." [transcript, VXgusdDbsQE @00:34–01:29] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VXgusdDbsQE)
- **On agent hype:** People "waste 200, 300, $1,000 on AI credits, and then it collapses… because they don't know what good looks like… So how are they going to be the manager or the CEO of the agents?" [transcript, VXgusdDbsQE @04:08] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VXgusdDbsQE)
- **AI only works with context:** AI never gives the best answer out of the box. Have ChatGPT write a book and it will, but it won't be worth reading. Past a certain level of context, AI isn't supplying knowledge but doing chores. He predicts the future digital product is education plus AI prompts for execution, merging DIY/DWY/DFY. [ZH summary] — [letters.thedankoe.com/p/how-smart-creators-will-make-money](https://letters.thedankoe.com/p/how-smart-creators-will-make-money)

## 6. Funnel, monetization & philosophy ("one-person business," "write to think")

### Takeaway
His model is to write content, get traffic, and feed it into his own products. The ladder runs e-book, course, cohort, community. By 2026 he argues info products have hit "final stage" market sophistication and are being replaced by education plus AI execution software (Eden), with bootcamps bundled into the software plan. His philosophy: writing is "the leverage that unlocks other forms of leverage"; writing is thinking ("forced linearity"); "you are the niche"; and creators form a "meaning economy."

### Cited Findings
- **The two-lever model:** "You write content, you get traffic, and depending on the amount of traffic you get and how good your product actually is, that results in the amount of money that you make." Product ladder: "a simple e-book. Level one, a course, level two, a cohort, level three, and a community level four." [transcript, VyR8nqD3sQ8 @03:43, @05:15] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VyR8nqD3sQ8)
- **Info products saturating:** Citing Eugene Schwartz's market-sophistication stages: "we've hit the final stage of market sophistication across the board with info products… the really, really good ones are still going to do really, really good… the average ones… are going to do really bad." [transcript, VyR8nqD3sQ8 @07:13–08:26] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VyR8nqD3sQ8)

That's 18 × $150 products a day, or 111 × $25 subscriptions a day, or one $5K client every other day, or one $10K client every 4 days. At a 2.5% landing-page conversion he needs about 720 visitors a day. He recommends beginners start with client work, then move to products. [transcript, VXgusdDbsQE @05:45–08:41] — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VXgusdDbsQE); same in letter — [letters.thedankoe.com/p/how-to-start-a-one-person-business](https://letters.thedankoe.com/p/how-to-start-a-one-person-business)
